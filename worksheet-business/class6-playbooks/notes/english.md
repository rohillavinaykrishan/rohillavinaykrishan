## 1. Sentences

- A **sentence** is a group of words that makes **complete sense**. It begins with a **capital letter** and ends with **. ? or !**
- **Subject:** who or what the sentence is about. **Predicate:** what is said about the subject.
  *Riya* (subject) | *plays the guitar beautifully.* (predicate)

### Kinds of sentences

| Kind | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Declarative (assertive)** | Makes a statement | Mumbai is a busy city. |
| **Interrogative** | Asks a question | Where is the Gateway of India? |
| **Imperative** | Gives a command or request | Please close the door. |
| **Exclamatory** | Shows strong feeling | What a beautiful sunset! |

- **Phrase:** a group of words **without** a subject and verb (*in the morning*). **Clause:** has a subject and verb (*when the bell rang*).
- **Simple sentence:** one clause. **Compound sentence:** two main clauses joined by *and, but, or, so*. **Complex sentence:** a main clause + a dependent clause (*I stayed home **because it was raining**.*).

:::trick
**Kinds of sentences: "DIIE = Dogs In India Eat"**: **D**eclarative, **I**nterrogative, **I**mperative, **E**xclamatory.
:::

:::check
1. Identify the kind: "Shut the window."
2. Underline the subject: "The tall boy in the blue shirt won the race."
3. Is "under the old banyan tree" a phrase or a clause?
4. Join into a compound sentence: "It was raining. We went out."
:::

## 2. Nouns

- A **noun** is a **naming word**: the name of a person, place, animal, thing or idea.

| Kind | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Proper** | A particular name (capital letter) | Riya, Mumbai, the Ganga |
| **Common** | A general name | girl, city, river |
| **Collective** | A group as one | a **herd** of cattle, a **swarm** of bees, a **bunch** of keys, a **fleet** of ships, a **pride** of lions, a **flock** of sheep |
| **Material** | A substance | gold, wood, cotton |
| **Abstract** | A quality, feeling or idea (can't be touched) | honesty, joy, childhood, bravery |

- **Countable nouns:** can be counted (book, chair). **Uncountable nouns:** can't be counted (water, advice, furniture).

### Gender

| Masculine | Feminine | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|---|
| king | queen | hero | heroine |
| lion | lioness | nephew | niece |
| horse | mare | bull | cow |
| actor | actress | monk | nun |

- **Common gender:** teacher, friend, child. **Neuter gender:** table, book.

### Number (plurals)

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Most nouns: add **-s** | book → books |
| **-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o**: add **-es** | bus → buses, box → boxes, potato → potatoes |
| Consonant + **y** → **-ies** | baby → babies (but boy → boys) |
| **-f / -fe** → **-ves** | leaf → leaves, knife → knives (but roof → roofs) |
| Irregular | man → men, child → children, mouse → mice, tooth → teeth, ox → oxen |
| Same form | sheep, deer, fish, aircraft |

### Possessive nouns

- Singular: add **'s** (the girl's bag). Plural ending in s: add only **'** (the girls' room). Irregular plural: **'s** (the children's park).

:::trick
**Abstract = "Can't touch, can only feel"**: love, fear, honesty, freedom.
**Collective nouns are a "team name"**: a **team** of players, a **class** of students, a **crowd** of people at Dadar station!
:::

:::play Noun Basket
The teacher calls out a kind of noun ("Collective!"). The ball is passed around, and whoever holds it must give an example in 5 seconds ("a swarm of bees!"). If they repeat one or are too slow, they're out.
:::

:::check
1. Pick out the abstract noun: "Her kindness won everyone's heart."
2. Write the collective noun: a ______ of fish, a ______ of stars.
3. Write the plurals: half, city, mango, ox.
4. Write the feminine of: nephew, tiger, host.
:::

## 3. Pronouns

- A **pronoun** is used **in place of a noun** to avoid repeating it.

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Personal** | I, we, you, he, she, it, they (subject); me, us, him, her, them (object) |
| **Possessive** | mine, ours, yours, his, hers, theirs |
| **Reflexive** | myself, yourself, himself, herself, ourselves, themselves |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those |
| **Interrogative** | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| **Relative** | who, whom, whose, which, that (they join clauses) |
| **Indefinite** | someone, anybody, everyone, nothing, all, few |

- **Subject vs object:** *She* helped *me*. (Not "Her helped I.")
- **Who** is for people, **which** is for things and animals, and **that** can be used for both.
- **Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe:** its, yours, hers (**it's** = it is!).

:::trick
**"It's" = "It is". "Its" = belongs to it.** Test: can you say "it is"? Then use **it's**.
:::

:::check
1. Fill in: The dog wagged ______ tail. (its / it's)
2. Correct: "Me and Riya went to the market."
3. Fill in a reflexive pronoun: "They enjoyed ______ at Juhu beach."
4. Use who or which: "The book ______ I bought is interesting."
:::

## 4. Adjectives

- An **adjective** describes a noun or pronoun (it tells us **what kind, how many, which**).

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Quality** | brave, tall, beautiful |
| **Quantity** | some, much, little, enough |
| **Number** | one, first, few, many, several |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those |
| **Interrogative** | which, what, whose (followed by a noun) |
| **Possessive** | my, your, his, her, our, their |

### Degrees of comparison

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| tall | taller | tallest |
| big | bigger | biggest |
| happy | happier | happiest |
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |
| good | better | best |
| bad | worse | worst |
| many / much | more | most |
| little | less | least |

- Use the comparative with **than** for **2 things**, and the superlative with **the** for **3 or more**.
  *Riya is taller **than** Aarav. She is **the tallest** in the class.*

### Order of adjectives

**Opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → purpose + noun**
*a lovely big old round red Indian wooden dining table*

:::trick
**Order: "OSASCOMP"**, chanted like "Oh-So-A-Scomp!": Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose.
:::

:::check
1. Write the comparative and superlative of: thin, bad, careful.
2. Pick out the adjectives: "The little girl wore a pretty pink frock."
3. Correct: "He is more taller than his brother."
4. Arrange: (cotton / white / a / new) shirt.
:::

## 5. Verbs

- A **verb** is an **action or being** word: run, eat, think, is, are, was.

### Kinds of verbs

| Kind | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Transitive** | Has an **object** (answers "what?" or "whom?") | She **reads** *a book*. |
| **Intransitive** | No object | The baby **sleeps**. |
| **Auxiliary (helping)** | Helps the main verb | She **is** dancing. They **have** finished. |
| **Modal** | Shows ability, permission, duty | can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would |
| **Linking** | Joins the subject to a description | She **seems** tired. |

### Finite and non-finite verbs

- **Finite:** changes with the subject and tense (*She **goes**, they **go**, she **went***).
- **Non-finite:** does **not** change: the **infinitive** (*to go*), the **gerund** (*Swimming is fun*) and the **participle** (*a **broken** cup*, the **running** boy).

### Forms of verbs

| Base | Past | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| go | went | gone |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| write | wrote | written |
| begin | began | begun |
| bring | brought | brought |
| buy | bought | bought |
| catch | caught | caught |
| drink | drank | drunk |
| swim | swam | swum |
| take | took | taken |

:::trick
**Transitive = "Transfer"**: the action **transfers** to an object. *She kicked → the ball.*
**Gerund = a verb + ing acting as a noun**: "**Reading** is my hobby."
:::

:::play Verb Statues
The teacher calls a verb ("Swim! Write! Dance!"). Students freeze in that action. Then the teacher says "Past participle!" and students shout "Swum! Written! Danced!"
:::

:::check
1. Transitive or intransitive? (a) The dog barked loudly. (b) Riya painted a picture.
2. Underline the gerund: "Cycling keeps you fit."
3. Write the past and past participle of: sing, choose, teach.
4. Pick out the modal verb: "You should wear a helmet."
:::

## 6. Tenses

| | **Simple** | **Continuous** | **Perfect** | **Perfect Continuous** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | I **play** / She **plays** | I **am playing** | I **have played** | I **have been playing** |
| **Past** | I **played** | I **was playing** | I **had played** | I **had been playing** |
| **Future** | I **will play** | I **will be playing** | I **will have played** | I **will have been playing** |

### When to use them

- **Simple present:** habits and facts. *The Sun **rises** in the east. She **goes** to school by bus.*
- **Present continuous:** happening now. *It **is raining** now.*
- **Present perfect:** just finished, or connected to now. *I **have finished** my homework.*
- **Simple past:** finished at a definite time. *We **visited** Elephanta Caves last year.*
- **Past continuous:** an action going on in the past. *I **was sleeping** when the phone rang.*
- **Past perfect:** the earlier of two past actions. *The train **had left** before we reached the station.*
- **Future:** *We **will go** to Lonavala tomorrow.*

### Signal words

| Tense | Signal words |
|---|---|
| Simple present | always, usually, every day, often |
| Present continuous | now, at present, look!, listen! |
| Present perfect | already, just, yet, ever, never, since, for |
| Simple past | yesterday, ago, last week, in 2020 |

- **Since** + a point in time (*since Monday*). **For** + a length of time (*for two hours*).

:::trick
**Tense = time + form**: "**Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous**" × "**Present, Past, Future**" = **12 tenses**, a 3 × 4 grid. Draw the grid from memory before every exam!
:::

:::play Tense Time Machine
Write a sentence on the board ("Riya eats a vada pav"). Students roll a die: 1–2 = past, 3–4 = present, 5–6 = future, then pick a card (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous) and change the sentence. Correct = 1 point.
:::

:::check
1. Change to the present continuous: "She writes a letter."
2. Fill in since or for: "I have lived in Mumbai ______ 2018."
3. Correct: "I have seen him yesterday."
4. Name the tense: "They had finished dinner before we arrived."
:::

## 7. Subject–Verb Agreement

- **A singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.**
  *The boy **runs**. The boys **run**.*

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Two subjects joined by **and** take a plural verb | Riya and Aarav **are** friends. |
| **Each, every, everyone, nobody, either, neither** take a singular verb | Each of the boys **has** a bag. |
| **Either…or / neither…nor**: the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** late. |
| A collective noun acting as one unit takes a singular verb | The team **is** practising. |
| **One of** + plural noun takes a singular verb | One of my friends **lives** in Thane. |
| **As well as / along with / with** don't change the subject | The captain, as well as his players, **was** happy. |
| Some nouns look plural but are singular | The news **is** good. Mathematics **is** easy. |
| A plural-only noun takes a plural verb | My scissors **are** sharp. The trousers **are** new. |

:::trick
**"Each, Every, Either, Neither, One = ONE"**: always singular!
:::

:::check
1. Choose: "One of the students (is / are) absent."
2. Choose: "Neither Riya nor her friends (was / were) present."
3. Choose: "Bread and butter (is / are) my breakfast." (one dish!)
4. Correct: "The news are very exciting."
:::

## 8. Adverbs, Prepositions and Conjunctions

### Adverbs (they describe a verb, an adjective or another adverb)

| Kind | Answers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Manner | How? | slowly, bravely, carefully |
| Time | When? | now, yesterday, soon |
| Place | Where? | here, there, outside |
| Frequency | How often? | always, never, often |
| Degree | How much? | very, too, almost |

### Prepositions (they show the relation of a noun to another word)

| Use | Prepositions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | **at** (exact time), **on** (days and dates), **in** (months, years, parts of the day) | at 7 o'clock, on Monday, in May, in the morning |
| **Place** | at, in, on, under, between, among, beside, behind | in Mumbai, on the table |
| **Movement** | into, onto, towards, across, through | She jumped **into** the pool. |

- **Between** for 2 things and **among** for more than 2. **Beside** means next to. **Besides** means in addition to.
- **Fixed prepositions:** good **at**, afraid **of**, interested **in**, angry **with** (a person), angry **at** (a thing), fond **of**, proud **of**, different **from**.

### Conjunctions (joining words)

- **Coordinating:** and, but, or, so, yet (**FANBOYS**: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so)
- **Subordinating:** because, although, when, while, if, unless, since, until
- **Correlative (in pairs):** either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, both…and

:::trick
**Time prepositions: "AT the clock, ON the calendar, IN the long time"**: at 5 pm, on Sunday, in 2026.
:::

:::check
1. Fill in: The match starts ______ 4 pm ______ Saturday.
2. Fill in between or among: "Divide the sweets ______ the three children."
3. Join using although: "He was tired. He finished his homework."
4. Pick out the adverb: "She sings beautifully."
:::

## 9. Articles and Determiners

- **A** comes before a **consonant sound**: a book, a **u**niform (the "yoo" sound), a **o**ne-rupee coin (the "wun" sound).
- **An** comes before a **vowel sound**: an apple, an **h**our (a silent h), an **M**LA (the "em" sound), an honest man.
- **The** is the definite article. Use it for specific things, unique things (the Sun), rivers, seas and mountain ranges (the Ganga, the Arabian Sea, the Himalayas), superlatives (the best), and musical instruments (play the guitar).
- **No article** before most names of people, cities and countries, meals (have lunch) or languages.
- **Determiners:** this, that, some, any, much, many, few, little, each, every
  - **Few / little** = hardly any (negative). **A few / a little** = some (positive).
  - **Much** goes with uncountable nouns (much water). **Many** goes with countable nouns (many books).

:::trick
**Go by the SOUND, not the letter!** "an hour" (the h is silent), "a university" (it sounds like "yoo").
:::

:::check
1. Fill in a/an/the: ______ European, ______ umbrella, ______ Sun.
2. Fill in much or many: "How ______ students are present?"
3. Correct: "She plays a piano very well."
4. Fill in few or a few: "He has ______ friends, so he is happy."
:::

## 10. Active and Passive Voice

- **Active voice:** the subject **does** the action. *Riya **wrote** a poem.*
- **Passive voice:** the subject **receives** the action. *A poem **was written** by Riya.*

**Steps:** (1) Swap the object and the subject. (2) Use the correct form of **be** + the **past participle**. (3) Add "**by**" + the doer (if needed).

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | She **cleans** the room. | The room **is cleaned** by her. |
| Present continuous | He **is painting** the wall. | The wall **is being painted** by him. |
| Simple past | They **built** a bridge. | A bridge **was built** by them. |
| Present perfect | I **have finished** the work. | The work **has been finished** by me. |
| Simple future | We **will plant** trees. | Trees **will be planted** by us. |
| Modal | You **must follow** the rules. | The rules **must be followed** by you. |

- Pronoun changes: I → me, we → us, he → him, she → her, they → them.

:::trick
**"Be + V3"**: every passive sentence has a form of **be** (is, am, are, was, were, been, being, be) + the **third form** of the verb.
:::

:::check
1. Change to passive: "The cat drank the milk."
2. Change to passive: "Riya is singing a song."
3. Change to passive: "The BMC will repair the road."
4. Change to active: "The window was broken by Aarav."
:::

## 11. Direct and Indirect (Reported) Speech

- **Direct:** the exact words, in quotation marks. *He said, "I am tired."*
- **Indirect:** reported in our own words. *He said that he was tired.*

### Rules

1. **If the reporting verb is in the past** (said), change the tense **one step back**:

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| am / is → was | are → were |
| simple present → simple past | will → would |
| present continuous → past continuous | can → could |
| simple past → past perfect | may → might |
| has / have → had | shall → should |

2. **Change the pronouns** to suit the speaker.
3. **Change words of time and place:** now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, here → there, this → that, ago → before.
4. **Questions:** use **asked**. For yes/no questions use **if / whether**. For wh-questions use the **wh-word**. The sentence becomes a statement (no question mark).
   *She said to me, "Where do you live?"* → *She asked me where I lived.*
5. **Commands and requests:** use **told / ordered / requested** + **to** + the verb.
   *The teacher said, "Open your books."* → *The teacher told us to open our books.*
6. **Universal truths don't change tense:** *He said, "The Earth is round."* → *He said that the Earth **is** round.*

:::trick
**"One step back in time"**: present → past, past → past perfect. Think of a **time machine going one step back**!
:::

:::play Gossip Reporter
Student A whispers a sentence ("I am going to Juhu tomorrow"). Student B reports it to the class in indirect speech ("A said that she was going to Juhu the next day"). The class checks the changes.
:::

:::check
1. Change to indirect: She said, "I like mangoes."
2. Change to indirect: He said to me, "Are you coming tomorrow?"
3. Change to indirect: The mother said, "Drink your milk."
4. Change to indirect: The teacher said, "The Sun rises in the east."
:::

## 12. Punctuation

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital letter** | The start of a sentence, proper nouns, "I" | My friend Riya lives in Pune. |
| **Full stop (.)** | The end of a statement or command | We won the match. |
| **Question mark (?)** | The end of a direct question | Where are you going? |
| **Exclamation mark (!)** | Strong feeling | Hurray! We won! |
| **Comma (,)** | Lists, after "yes" or "no", before quotations, with addresses | I bought apples, mangoes and grapes. |
| **Apostrophe (')** | Possession and short forms | Riya's bag, don't, it's |
| **Quotation marks (" ")** | Exact spoken words | He said, "Come here." |
| **Colon (:)** | Before a list or explanation | You need three things: a pen, a book and a ruler. |
| **Hyphen (-)** | Joins words | well-known, twenty-one |

:::check
Punctuate: **riya said to her mother can i go to the gateway of india with my friends on sunday**
:::

## 13. Vocabulary Builder

### Synonyms (same meaning)

| Word | Synonym | Word | Synonym |
|---|---|---|---|
| big | large, huge | begin | start, commence |
| brave | courageous, bold | end | finish, conclude |
| happy | glad, joyful | quick | fast, rapid |
| sad | unhappy, sorrowful | help | assist, aid |
| beautiful | lovely, pretty | angry | furious, cross |
| clever | intelligent, smart | old | ancient, aged |

### Antonyms (opposite meaning)

| Word | Antonym | Word | Antonym |
|---|---|---|---|
| ancient | modern | victory | defeat |
| generous | selfish, stingy | arrive | depart |
| polite | rude | increase | decrease |
| success | failure | import | export |
| strong | weak | possible | impossible |
| honest | dishonest | visible | invisible |

### Homophones (same sound, different meaning)

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **their** (belongs to them) / **there** (a place) / **they're** (they are) | **to** / **too** (also, very) / **two** (2) | **hear** (with ears) / **here** (this place) |
| **peace** (calm) / **piece** (a part) | **write** / **right** | **weak** / **week** |
| **flour** (atta) / **flower** | **sea** / **see** | **son** / **sun** |

### Prefixes and suffixes

- **Prefixes (at the start):** un- (unhappy), dis- (dislike), im- (impossible), in- (incorrect), re- (rewrite), pre- (preview), mis- (misbehave)
- **Suffixes (at the end):** -ful (careful), -less (careless), -ness (kindness), -ment (enjoyment), -ly (quickly), -er/-or (teacher, actor)

### Idioms

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A piece of cake | Very easy |
| Break the ice | Start a conversation |
| Once in a blue moon | Very rarely |
| Hit the nail on the head | Say exactly the right thing |
| Under the weather | Feeling unwell |
| Let the cat out of the bag | Reveal a secret |
| Cry over spilt milk | Be sad about something that can't be changed |
| Burn the midnight oil | Study or work late at night |

## 14. Comprehension (Reading Skills)

### The 5-step method

1. **Read the passage once** for the general idea.
2. **Read the questions** carefully.
3. **Read the passage again** and **underline** the answers.
4. Answer in **complete sentences**, **in your own words** where asked.
5. For "word meaning" questions, find the word in the passage and read the **whole sentence** for context.

### Types of questions

- **Factual:** the answer is directly in the passage.
- **Inference:** "Why do you think…?" Read between the lines.
- **Vocabulary:** find a word that means… / give the meaning of…
- **Title:** short (3–5 words) and covers the **main idea**.
- **True or false, MCQs, and summary (in a fixed number of words).**

:::exam
Never copy whole paragraphs. Write **only what is asked**. In vocabulary questions, the word you give must be the **same part of speech** as the one asked for (a noun for a noun, a verb for a verb).
:::

## 15. Composition (Essays and Stories)

### Types

| Type | Example topic | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | "A visit to Marine Drive", "My favourite festival: Ganesh Chaturthi" | Use the **5 senses** (see, hear, smell, taste, touch) |
| **Narrative / story** | "The day I got lost", "Complete the story beginning with…" | Beginning → problem → climax → ending + a moral or feeling |
| **Argumentative / opinion** | "Should mobile phones be allowed in school?" | Your opinion + 2–3 reasons + a conclusion |
| **Personal experience** | "My first day in a new school" | Feelings, details, what you learned |
| **Picture composition** | A picture is given | Describe it, or write a story connected to it |

### Structure (about 200–250 words in Class 6)

1. **Introduction** (2–3 lines): an interesting start, such as a question, a quote or a sound.
2. **Body** (2–3 paragraphs): one idea per paragraph, with details and examples.
3. **Conclusion** (2–3 lines): a feeling, a lesson or a message.

:::trick
**A great opening = "Q-Q-S"**: start with a **Q**uestion ("Have you ever seen the sea glowing at night?"), a **Q**uote, or a **S**ound ("Ding! Ding! The school bell rang…").
:::

"A ride in a Mumbai local train", "Ganpati Visarjan at Girgaon Chowpatty", "A rainy day in Mumbai", "A visit to the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (museum)", "The dabbawalas of Mumbai"
:::

:::

## 16. Letter Writing

### Informal letter (to family and friends)

```
Sender's address
Date

Dear Grandma,                       ← Salutation

Para 1: Greeting + why you are writing
Para 2–3: Main news / details
Para 4: Closing lines (ask about them, send regards)

With love,                          ← Complimentary close
Riya                                ← Your name
```

### Formal letter (to the principal, an editor, an officer)

```
Sender's address
Date

The Principal / The Ward Officer      ← Receiver's designation
School name / Office name
Address

Subject: Request for … (one line)

Respected Sir/Madam,  (or Dear Sir/Madam,)

Para 1: Introduce yourself + reason for writing
Para 2: Details of the problem/request
Para 3: What you want done + thanks

Yours faithfully, (or Yours sincerely, if you know the name)
Riya Sharma
Class 6 B
```

| | Informal | Formal |
|---|---|---|
| Language | Friendly, personal | Polite, to the point |
| Subject line | No | **Yes** |
| Close | With love / Yours lovingly / Your loving | **Yours faithfully / Yours sincerely** |

:::trick
**"F for Formal, F for Faithfully."** When you don't know the person's name (Sir/Madam), end with **Yours faithfully**.
:::

## 18. English Literature Toolkit

The literature textbook **differs from school to school**. Use this toolkit for **every** prose lesson and poem in your book.

### For every prose lesson, make a "Story Card"

| Box | What to write |
|---|---|
| Title and author | |
| Setting | Where and when the story happens |
| Characters | Main characters + 2 qualities each, with one example |
| Plot in 5 lines | Beginning → problem → events → climax → ending |
| Theme or message | What the story teaches (honesty, friendship, courage…) |
| 5 new words | With meanings |
| 3 important quotes | Who said them, to whom, and why |

### For every poem, make a "Poem Card"

| Box | What to write |
|---|---|
| Title and poet | |
| What the poem is about (3 lines) | |
| Mood or tone | Happy, sad, peaceful, patriotic, humorous… |
| Rhyme scheme | For example **aabb** or **abab** (mark the line endings with letters) |
| Poetic devices | See the table below, with one line from the poem as an example |
| Message | |

### Poetic devices

| Device | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simile** | A comparison using **like** or **as** | as brave **as** a lion; she sings **like** a nightingale |
| **Metaphor** | A direct comparison (no like or as) | Time **is** a thief. |
| **Personification** | Giving human qualities to non-living things | The wind **whispered**. |
| **Alliteration** | The same first sound repeated | **S**ally **s**ells **s**ea **s**hells |
| **Onomatopoeia** | Words that sound like their meaning | buzz, splash, tick-tock |
| **Repetition** | A word or line repeated for effect | "Miles to go before I sleep, and miles to go before I sleep" |
| **Hyperbole** | Exaggeration | I've told you **a million times**! |

### Answering "reference to context" (extract) questions

1. **Who** is speaking, to **whom**? 2. **Where** and **when**? 3. **What** does the line mean? 4. **Why** was it said? 5. **What happens next**?

### Character sketch format

**Introduction** (who they are) → **3 qualities**, each with an **example from the text** → **conclusion** (what we learn from them).

:::trick
**Simile vs metaphor: "Simile is Similar (with like or as). Metaphor is a Magic transformation (it *is* the thing)."**
:::

:::play Poetry Detective
Give each team a poem from your textbook. They must find one simile, one metaphor, one example of personification and one of alliteration, and work out the rhyme scheme, in 7 minutes. Each correct find scores a point!
:::

:::exam
**Teacher: please share the names of your school's prose lessons and poems.** Story Cards and Poem Cards can then be made for each one, using this toolkit, for the final exam.
:::
