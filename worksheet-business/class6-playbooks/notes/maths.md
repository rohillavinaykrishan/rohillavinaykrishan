## 1. Knowing Our Numbers

### Indian and International systems

| Indian | Digits | International |
|---|---|---|
| Thousand (1,000) | 4 | Thousand (1,000) |
| Lakh (1,00,000) | 6 | Hundred thousand (100,000) |
| Ten lakh (10,00,000) | 7 | Million (1,000,000) |
| Crore (1,00,00,000) | 8 | Ten million (10,000,000) |
| Ten crore | 9 | Hundred million |
| Arab (100 crore) | 10 | Billion |

- **Indian commas:** the first comma comes after 3 digits from the right, then after every 2 digits: **5,23,47,816**
- **International commas:** a comma after every 3 digits: **52,347,816**

:::trick
**Indian = 3-2-2-2** (like the Mumbai local: first a big compartment, then small ones).
**International = 3-3-3** (like an escalator, all steps are equal).
**10 lakh = 1 million**, and **1 crore = 10 million**.
:::

### Place value and face value

- **Face value** = the digit itself. **Place value** = digit × its place.
- In 4,**7**3,215 the face value of 7 is **7**, and its place value is **70,000**.

### Estimation (rounding off)

- Look at the digit just to the right of the place you are rounding to. If it is **5 or more, round up**. If it is **less than 5, round down**.
- 4,672 to the nearest hundred → look at 7 → round up → **4,700**
- 83,419 to the nearest thousand → look at 4 → round down → **83,000**

:::play Number Antakshari
One student says a big number, like "6,45,302". The next student must say it in words, then give a new number. If they say it wrong, they sit down. Play one round in the Indian system and one in the International system.
:::

### Roman numerals

| I | V | X | L | C | D | M |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 10 | 50 | 100 | 500 | 1000 |

- A smaller numeral **before** a bigger one means subtract: IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90
- A smaller numeral **after** a bigger one means add: VI = 6, LX = 60
- I, X, C and M can repeat up to 3 times. **V, L and D never repeat.**

:::trick
"**I V**alue **X**-tra **L**arge **C**old **D**rinks **M**ore": that's the order I, V, X, L, C, D, M.
:::

:::check
1. Write 7,08,45,000 in words (Indian system).
2. Insert commas in 4567890 using the International system.
3. Round off 56,749 to the nearest thousand.
4. Write 94 in Roman numerals.
:::

## 2. Whole Numbers, Integers and the Number Line

### Kinds of numbers

- **Natural numbers (N):** 1, 2, 3, … (counting numbers, with no 0)
- **Whole numbers (W):** 0, 1, 2, 3, … (natural numbers + 0)
- **Integers (Z):** …, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …
- The **successor** of a number is that number + 1, and its **predecessor** is that number − 1. 0 has no predecessor among whole numbers.

### Properties (whole numbers)

| Property | Addition | Multiplication |
|---|---|---|
| Closure | Always a whole number ✔ | ✔ |
| Commutative | a + b = b + a | a × b = b × a |
| Associative | (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) | (a × b) × c = a × (b × c) |
| Identity | a + 0 = a | a × 1 = a |
| Distributive | a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c | |

- Subtraction and division are **not** commutative: 5 − 3 ≠ 3 − 5.
- Division by 0 is **not defined**.

### Integers on the number line

- Numbers to the right are bigger. **−2 > −7**, because −2 is closer to the right.
- **Adding** a positive number means moving right. **Adding** a negative number means moving left.
- **Subtracting** an integer means adding its opposite: 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = **8**

| Multiply or divide | Result |
|---|---|
| (+) × (+) | + |
| (−) × (−) | + |
| (+) × (−) or (−) × (+) | − |

:::trick
**"A friend of a friend is a friend. An enemy of an enemy is a friend."**
(+)(+) = +,  (−)(−) = +,  but a friend with an enemy (+)(−) = −
:::

:::mumbai Temperature and lift buttons
A Mumbai building has floors B2, B1 (basements), G, 1, 2, 3 … That's an integer line! B2 = −2, G = 0. Going from B2 up to the 3rd floor = 3 − (−2) = **5 floors**.
:::

:::play Human Number Line
Stick cards from −10 to +10 on the floor. Give a student a sum like "−3 + 5". They stand on −3 and walk 5 steps right, landing on +2. The class checks the answer.
:::

:::check
1. Write the predecessor of 1,00,000.
2. Find: (−12) + 7 − (−5)
3. Find: (−6) × (−4) and (−36) ÷ 9
4. Arrange in ascending order: 3, −8, 0, −1, 5
:::

## 3. Divisibility, Factors, HCF and LCM

### Divisibility rules

| Divisible by | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | The last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 | 538 |
| 3 | The sum of the digits is divisible by 3 | 471 → 4 + 7 + 1 = 12 |
| 4 | The last 2 digits are divisible by 4 | 3,716 → 16 |
| 5 | The last digit is 0 or 5 | 945 |
| 6 | Divisible by both 2 and 3 | 312 |
| 8 | The last 3 digits are divisible by 8 | 5,120 → 120 |
| 9 | The sum of the digits is divisible by 9 | 729 → 18 |
| 10 | The last digit is 0 | 670 |
| 11 | (sum of digits in odd places) − (sum in even places) = 0 or a multiple of 11 | 2,728 → (8 + 7) − (2 + 2) = 11 |

### Factors and multiples

- A **factor** divides a number exactly. A **multiple** is the number × 1, 2, 3, …
- **Prime:** exactly 2 factors (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, …). **Composite:** more than 2 factors.
- **1 is neither prime nor composite.** **2 is the only even prime.**
- **Co-primes:** numbers whose HCF is 1 (8 and 15). **Twin primes:** primes that differ by 2 (11 and 13).
- **Prime factorisation:** 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5

### HCF and LCM

- **HCF** (Highest Common Factor) is the biggest number that divides all of them.
- **LCM** (Lowest Common Multiple) is the smallest number that all of them divide into.
- **The golden rule (for 2 numbers):** HCF × LCM = product of the numbers

**Example:** 12 = 2 × 2 × 3, 18 = 2 × 3 × 3
HCF = 2 × 3 = **6** (common factors, lowest powers). LCM = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = **36** (all factors, highest powers).
Check: 6 × 36 = 216 = 12 × 18 ✔

:::trick
**HCF = "share"** (it's about dividing things into equal groups, like ribbons cut into equal pieces).
**LCM = "meet again"** (it's about things that repeat, like two bells or two buses meeting again).
:::

:::exam
Word problems: if the question says "**largest** size / **maximum** number of equal pieces", use **HCF**. If it says "**together again** / **least** number / **at the same time**", use **LCM**.
:::

:::mumbai
Two Mumbai local trains leave Churchgate together. One returns every 12 minutes and the other every 18 minutes. When will they leave together again? LCM(12, 18) = **36 minutes**.
:::

:::play Fizz-Buzz Primes
Count around the class. On a multiple of 3 say "Fizz", on a multiple of 5 say "Buzz", and on both say "Fizz-Buzz". Round 2: clap on every prime number.
:::

:::check
1. Is 5,148 divisible by 4? By 9? By 11?
2. Write the prime factorisation of 84.
3. Find the HCF and LCM of 24 and 36.
4. The HCF of two numbers is 4 and their LCM is 48. One number is 16. Find the other.
:::

## 4. Sets

- A **set** is a well-defined collection of objects. "Good students" is **not** well-defined. "Vowels in English" **is** well-defined.
- Elements are written in curly brackets: A = {a, e, i, o, u}
- **∈** means "belongs to" and **∉** means "does not belong to": e ∈ A, b ∉ A
- **Roster form:** {2, 4, 6, 8}. **Set-builder form:** {x : x is an even number, x < 10}
- **Cardinal number n(A)** is the number of elements: n({a, e, i, o, u}) = 5

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Empty (null) set ∅ or { } | No elements | Months with 32 days |
| Singleton | Exactly 1 element | {0} |
| Finite | Elements can be counted | Days of the week |
| Infinite | Elements cannot be counted to the end | Natural numbers |
| Equal sets | Exactly the same elements | {1, 2, 3} = {3, 1, 2} |
| Equivalent sets | The same number of elements | {a, b} and {5, 9} |
| Subset ⊆ | Every element of A is in B | {1, 2} ⊆ {1, 2, 3} |

- Repeated elements are written **once**: the letters of "MUMBAI" = {M, U, B, A, I}, so n = 5

:::trick
**Equal = same members. Equivalent = same count.** ("Equal twins, equivalent teams")
**{0} is NOT empty.** It has one element, 0.
:::

:::play Set Sorting Race
Write 20 words on the board (mango, Pune, 7, tiger, Nagpur, 13…). Teams race to sort them into sets: {fruits}, {cities in Maharashtra}, {prime numbers}, {animals}. Then each team writes one set in roster form and set-builder form.
:::

:::check
1. Write the set of letters of the word "ELEPHANT" in roster form, and find n(A).
2. Is {0} an empty set? Why?
3. Write in set-builder form: {1, 4, 9, 16, 25}
4. Are A = {p, q, r} and B = {1, 2, 3} equal or equivalent?
:::

## 5. Ratio, Proportion and Unitary Method

### Ratio

- A **ratio** compares two quantities **of the same kind and in the same unit**: a : b = a/b
- Always simplify: 24 : 36 = **2 : 3** (divide by the HCF, 12)
- **Convert to the same unit first:** 50 paise : ₹2 = 50 : 200 = **1 : 4**
- A ratio has **no unit**.
- **Dividing in a ratio:** share ₹600 between A and B in the ratio 2 : 3. Total parts = 5, so A = 2/5 × 600 = **₹240** and B = **₹360**

### Proportion

- Four numbers are in proportion if a : b = c : d, written **a : b :: c : d**
- **Product of extremes = product of means:** a × d = b × c
- Are 3, 5, 12, 20 in proportion? 3 × 20 = 60 and 5 × 12 = 60 ✔
- **Continued proportion:** a : b = b : c, so b² = a × c (b is the **mean proportional**)

### Unitary method

- Find the value of **ONE** first, then of **many**.
- 6 pens cost ₹90. So 1 pen = 90 ÷ 6 = ₹15, and 10 pens = 15 × 10 = **₹150**

:::trick
**"Extremes on the outside, means in the middle"**: a : **b :: c** : d. Outside × outside = inside × inside.
**Unitary method = "One, then Many"** (like finding the price of 1 vada pav before buying for the whole class!)
:::

:::mumbai
A BEST bus pass costs ₹750 for 30 days. What is the cost per day? 750 ÷ 30 = **₹25 per day**.
:::

:::play Recipe Scaling
"A recipe for 4 people needs 2 cups of rice. How much rice do we need for 10 people?" Teams scale a full recipe (pav bhaji!) up or down using the unitary method. The fastest correct team wins.
:::

:::check
1. Simplify the ratio 45 minutes : 2 hours.
2. Divide ₹1,400 between Riya and Aarav in the ratio 3 : 4.
3. Are 4, 6, 10, 15 in proportion?
4. 8 notebooks cost ₹240. What is the cost of 13 notebooks?
:::

## 6. Fractions

- **Proper:** numerator < denominator (3/7). **Improper:** numerator ≥ denominator (9/4). **Mixed:** 2 1/4
- **Converting:** 9/4 = 2 1/4 (9 ÷ 4 = 2, remainder 1), and 3 2/5 = (3 × 5 + 2)/5 = 17/5
- **Equivalent fractions:** multiply or divide the top and bottom by the same number: 2/3 = 4/6 = 8/12
- **Lowest terms:** divide by the HCF: 18/24 = **3/4**
- **Like fractions** have the same denominator. **Unlike fractions** have different denominators.

### Comparing

- Like fractions: the bigger numerator is bigger.
- Unlike fractions: make the denominators the same using the LCM, or cross-multiply. For 3/5 vs 2/3: 3 × 3 = 9 and 5 × 2 = 10, so **3/5 < 2/3**

### Operations

| Operation | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add or subtract | Make the denominators equal (LCM), then add or subtract the numerators | 3/4 + 1/6 = 9/12 + 2/12 = 11/12 |
| Multiply | top × top, bottom × bottom | 2/3 × 9/4 = 18/12 = 3/2 |
| Divide | Multiply by the reciprocal | 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 3/2 |
| "of" | Means multiply | 2/5 of 30 = 12 |

### BODMAS (order of operations)

**B**rackets → **O**f → **D**ivision → **M**ultiplication → **A**ddition → **S**ubtraction
Brackets order: ( ) first, then { }, then [ ]

:::trick
**Dividing fractions: "Keep, Change, Flip."** Keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second.
**BODMAS: "Big Old Dogs Must Always Sleep."**
:::

:::play Pizza Fractions
Draw 4 big circles (pizzas). Students colour slices to show 1/2, 2/4, 3/6 and 4/8, and discover they are all equal. Then "order pizzas" for the class: 3/4 + 1/2 of a pizza = how much?
:::

:::check
1. Convert 29/6 into a mixed fraction.
2. Reduce 36/48 to its lowest terms.
3. Find: 2 1/3 + 1 3/4
4. Simplify: 1/2 + 3/4 × 2/3 (use BODMAS)
:::

## 7. Decimals, Percent and Speed

### Decimal fractions

- 0.7 = 7/10, 0.07 = 7/100, 0.007 = 7/1000
- **Place value:** in 23.456, 4 is tenths, 5 is hundredths and 6 is thousandths
- **Fraction to decimal:** divide (3/4 = 0.75), or make the denominator 10 or 100 (3/4 = 75/100 = 0.75)
- **Decimal to fraction:** 0.45 = 45/100 = **9/20**
- **Adding or subtracting:** line up the decimal points (fill gaps with 0s)
- **Multiplying:** multiply as normal, then count the total decimal places: 1.2 × 0.3 = 0.36
- **× 10, 100, 1000:** move the point right by 1, 2, 3 places. **÷ 10, 100, 1000:** move it left.

### Percent

- **Percent = "out of 100"**: 25% = 25/100 = 1/4 = 0.25
- **Fraction to percent:** × 100 (3/5 × 100 = 60%)
- **Percent of a number:** 15% of 240 = 15/100 × 240 = **36**
- **What percent is a of b?** (a ÷ b) × 100. For 18 out of 24: 18/24 × 100 = **75%**

| Fraction | 1/2 | 1/4 | 3/4 | 1/5 | 1/10 | 1/3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Percent | 50% | 25% | 75% | 20% | 10% | 33 1/3% |

### Speed, distance and time

- **Speed = Distance ÷ Time.** **Distance = Speed × Time.** **Time = Distance ÷ Speed.**
- **km/h to m/s:** × 5/18. **m/s to km/h:** × 18/5. For example, 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = **20 m/s**

:::trick
**The DST triangle:** write **D** on top and **S** and **T** below it. Cover the one you want:
D = S × T,  S = D ÷ T,  T = D ÷ S
:::

:::mumbai
The Mumbai–Pune distance is about 150 km. A car takes 3 hours. Speed = 150 ÷ 3 = **50 km/h**.
:::

:::play Shop Discount Game
Make price tags for things like "School bag ₹800, 25% OFF". Students act as shoppers and work out the final price. The best saver wins.
:::

:::check
1. Convert 0.125 into a fraction in its lowest terms.
2. Find: 4.6 + 0.38 + 12.05
3. A student scored 42 out of 60. Find the percentage.
4. Convert 54 km/h into m/s.
:::

## 8. Algebra: Expressions and Substitution

### Basic terms

- **Variable:** a letter that stands for a number (x, y, a). **Constant:** a fixed number (5, −3).
- **Term:** a part of an expression separated by + or −. In 3x + 2y − 7 there are **3 terms**.
- **Coefficient:** the number multiplied by the variable. In −5ab, the coefficient is **−5**.
- **Like terms:** the same variables with the same powers (3x² and −7x²). **Unlike terms:** 3x and 3y.
- **Monomial** = 1 term, **binomial** = 2 terms, **trinomial** = 3 terms, **polynomial** = many terms
- **Degree:** the highest power of the variable. 4x³ + 2x − 1 has degree **3**.

### Operations

- You can add or subtract **only like terms**: 5x + 3x − 2x = **6x**, and 3a + 4b + 2a = **5a + 4b**
- **Multiplying:** multiply the numbers, then add the powers: 3x² × 4x³ = **12x⁵**
- **Dividing:** divide the numbers, then subtract the powers: 12a⁵ ÷ 3a² = **4a³**
- **Removing brackets:** a minus sign before a bracket changes every sign inside: −(2x − 3) = −2x + 3

### Framing expressions

| Words | Expression |
|---|---|
| 5 more than x | x + 5 |
| 3 less than y | y − 3 |
| Twice a number | 2n |
| A number divided by 4 | n/4 |
| 7 subtracted from 3 times p | 3p − 7 |

### Substitution

If a = 2 and b = −3: 3a + 2b = 3(2) + 2(−3) = 6 − 6 = **0**

:::trick
**Like terms are like twins: same letters, same powers.** Only twins can be added together!
:::

:::play Algebra Tiles Shopping
Mango = m, banana = b. "Riya buys 3 mangoes and 2 bananas, and Aarav buys 1 mango and 4 bananas. Together?" (4m + 6b). Then: "A mango costs ₹20 and a banana costs ₹5. Find the total cost." (substitution: ₹110)
:::

:::check
1. Write the coefficient of x in −7xy.
2. Simplify: 8a − 3b + 2a + 5b
3. Write an expression: "4 less than twice x".
4. If x = 3 and y = −2, find 2x² − 3y.
:::

## 9. Simple Linear Equations

- An **equation** is a statement that two expressions are equal: 2x + 3 = 11
- **Linear** means the highest power of the variable is 1.
- **Solving:** do the **same** thing to both sides, or **transpose** (move a term across and change its sign).

| Move across the = sign | It becomes |
|---|---|
| + | − |
| − | + |
| × | ÷ |
| ÷ | × |

**Example 1:** 5x − 7 = 3x + 9 → 5x − 3x = 9 + 7 → 2x = 16 → **x = 8**
**Example 2:** x/3 + 2 = 6 → x/3 = 4 → **x = 12**
**Always check:** put the answer back in. For example 1: 5(8) − 7 = 33 and 3(8) + 9 = 33 ✔

### Word problems: four steps

1. Let the unknown be **x**.
2. Write the equation from the words.
3. Solve it.
4. Check the answer and write it in a sentence.

Example: "A number is doubled and 6 is added. The result is 30." 2x + 6 = 30 → 2x = 24 → **x = 12**

:::trick
**"Cross the bridge, change your clothes!"** When a term crosses the = bridge, its sign changes.
:::

:::play Mystery Number Detective
The teacher thinks of a number: "I multiply it by 3, then subtract 4, and I get 11. What is my number?" Students write the equation (3x − 4 = 11) and solve it (x = 5). Then students take turns making their own riddles.
:::

:::check
1. Solve: 4x + 5 = 29
2. Solve: 7x − 3 = 4x + 12
3. Solve: (x − 2)/5 = 3
4. The sum of a number and 15 is 42. Find the number.
:::

## 10. Geometry: Lines and Angles

### Basic terms

- **Point:** shows a position, has no size. **Line:** extends endlessly both ways. **Ray:** one end point, extends one way. **Line segment:** two end points, has a fixed length.
- **Collinear points** lie on the same line. **Intersecting lines** meet at one point. **Parallel lines** never meet (∥).
- **Concurrent lines:** three or more lines that pass through the same point.

### Types of angles

| Angle | Measure |
|---|---|
| Acute | Less than 90° |
| Right | Exactly 90° |
| Obtuse | Between 90° and 180° |
| Straight | Exactly 180° |
| Reflex | Between 180° and 360° |
| Complete | Exactly 360° |

### Pairs of angles

- **Complementary:** add up to **90°** (30° and 60°)
- **Supplementary:** add up to **180°** (110° and 70°)
- **Adjacent angles:** share a common arm and vertex
- **Linear pair:** adjacent angles on a straight line, which add up to **180°**
- **Vertically opposite angles** are **equal** (when two lines cross)
- **Parallel lines and a transversal:** corresponding angles are equal, alternate angles are equal, and co-interior angles add up to 180°

:::trick
**C comes before S, and 90 comes before 180.** **C**omplementary = 90°, **S**upplementary = 180°.
Or: **C**omplementary makes a **C**orner (90°). **S**upplementary makes a **S**traight line (180°).
:::

:::play Body Angles
The teacher calls out "Acute!", "Right!", "Obtuse!", "Straight!" Students show the angle with their arms. Anyone who is wrong becomes the next caller.
:::

:::check
1. Find the complement of 35°.
2. Find the supplement of 128°.
3. Two angles form a linear pair. One is 3 times the other. Find both.
4. Name the angle: 270°.
:::

## 11. Triangles, Quadrilaterals, Polygons and Circles

### Triangles

- **By sides:** equilateral (all 3 sides equal), isosceles (2 sides equal), scalene (no sides equal)
- **By angles:** acute-angled, right-angled, obtuse-angled
- **Angle sum property:** the three angles add up to **180°**
- **Exterior angle** = the sum of the two **interior opposite** angles
- The sum of any two sides is always **greater** than the third side.
- In an equilateral triangle, each angle = **60°**

### Quadrilaterals (4 sides, angle sum = 360°)

| Shape | Special property |
|---|---|
| Parallelogram | Opposite sides are parallel and equal, and opposite angles are equal |
| Rectangle | All angles are 90°, and the diagonals are equal |
| Square | All sides are equal, all angles are 90°, and the diagonals are equal and cross at 90° |
| Rhombus | All sides are equal, and the diagonals cross at 90° |
| Trapezium | Exactly one pair of parallel sides |
| Kite | Two pairs of equal adjacent sides |

### Polygons

- Triangle (3 sides), quadrilateral (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6), heptagon (7), octagon (8), nonagon (9), decagon (10)
- **Angle sum of a polygon = (n − 2) × 180°.** For a hexagon: (6 − 2) × 180° = **720°**
- **Regular polygon:** all sides and all angles are equal

### Circle

- **Centre**, **radius** (from the centre to the circle), **diameter** = 2 × radius (passes through the centre)
- **Chord:** joins two points on the circle. The diameter is the **longest chord**.
- **Arc:** a part of the circle. **Semicircle:** half a circle. **Sector:** a "pizza slice". **Segment:** the part between a chord and an arc.
- **Circumference:** the distance around the circle

:::trick
**Polygon names: "Tri-Quad-Penta-Hexa-Hepta-Octa-Nona-Deca"**, said like a rap: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10!
**A square is a special rectangle AND a special rhombus.** It belongs to both families.
:::

:::mumbai Shapes in our city
The Gateway of India's arch is a semicircle on top. The Bandra–Worli Sea Link cables form triangles. Ask students: "Find 5 shapes on your way to school."
:::

:::play Shape Hunt
Teams get 5 minutes to find and list classroom objects for each shape: triangle, rectangle, square, circle, hexagon. Each correct object scores 1 point. A rare shape scores 2 points.
:::

:::check
1. Two angles of a triangle are 65° and 45°. Find the third.
2. Find the angle sum of an octagon.
3. Three angles of a quadrilateral are 80°, 95° and 110°. Find the fourth.
4. The radius of a circle is 7 cm. What is its diameter?
:::

## 12. Symmetry and Solids

### Symmetry

- A **line of symmetry** divides a figure into two **mirror halves**.

| Figure | Lines of symmetry |
|---|---|
| Equilateral triangle | 3 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 |
| Scalene triangle | 0 |
| Square | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 |
| Rhombus | 2 |
| Regular hexagon | 6 |
| Circle | Infinite |

- **Letters with symmetry:** A, H, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y (vertical line); B, C, D, E, K (horizontal line)

### Solids (3-D shapes)

| Solid | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube | 6 | 12 | 8 |
| Cuboid | 6 | 12 | 8 |
| Triangular prism | 5 | 9 | 6 |
| Square pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |
| Triangular pyramid (tetrahedron) | 4 | 6 | 4 |

- **Euler's formula: F + V − E = 2.** For a cube: 6 + 8 − 12 = 2 ✔

## 13. Mensuration: Perimeter and Area

### Perimeter (the boundary length, in cm, m, …)

| Shape | Perimeter |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | 2 × (length + breadth) |
| Square | 4 × side |
| Triangle | Sum of the three sides |
| Equilateral triangle | 3 × side |
| Regular polygon | Number of sides × side |

### Area (the space inside, in cm², m², …)

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | length × breadth |
| Square | side × side |
| Right-angled triangle | ½ × base × height |

- **Finding the missing side:** length = Area ÷ breadth, and side of a square = √Area
- **Units:** 1 m = 100 cm, but **1 m² = 10,000 cm²**
- **Cost problems:** cost = area (or perimeter) × rate
- **Path around a rectangle:** area of the path = (outer area) − (inner area)

**Example:** a room is 6 m × 4 m.
Perimeter = 2(6 + 4) = 20 m (for a border). Area = 6 × 4 = 24 m² (for tiles).
Tiling at ₹150 per m² = 24 × 150 = **₹3,600**

Fencing, border, lace, wire → perimeter. Tiles, paint, carpet, grass → area.

## 14. Data Handling, Mean and Median

### Collecting and organising data

- **Raw data:** data not yet arranged. An **array** is data arranged in ascending or descending order.
- **Tally marks:** |||| with a line across = 5, which makes counting easy
- A **frequency** table shows how many times each value occurs.

### Showing data

- **Pictograph:** uses pictures or symbols. Always give a **key** (🍎 = 10 apples).
- **Bar graph:** bars of **equal width** with **equal gaps**. The height shows the value. Give it a title, label the axes and choose a scale.

### Mean and median

- **Mean (average)** = sum of all the observations ÷ number of observations
  Mean of 4, 8, 6, 10, 12 = 40 ÷ 5 = **8**
- **Median** = the middle value after arranging the data in order
  - **Odd number of values:** the middle one. For 3, 7, 9, 12, 15 → **9**
  - **Even number of values:** the average of the two middle ones. For 2, 4, 6, 8 → (4 + 6) ÷ 2 = **5**
- **Range** = highest − lowest

:::trick
**Mean = "fair share"**: put everything together and share it equally.
**Median = "the middle of the road"**: line them up and stand in the middle. (Remember to **arrange first!**)
:::
