## 1. The Globe, Latitudes and Longitudes

- A **globe** is a **true model** of the Earth. It shows the correct shape, size and direction, but it isn't handy to carry around.
- The Earth's **axis** is an imaginary line through the **North and South Poles**. It is **tilted at 23½°** from the vertical.
- The Earth is not a perfect sphere. It is slightly flattened at the poles: a **geoid**.

### Latitudes (parallels)

- Imaginary **horizontal circles** drawn **parallel to the Equator**.
- The **Equator (0°)** divides the Earth into the **Northern and Southern Hemispheres**. It is the longest latitude.
- Latitudes get **smaller towards the poles**. The poles (90° N and 90° S) are just **points**.

| Important latitude | Degree |
|---|---|
| Equator | 0° |
| Tropic of Cancer | 23½° N |
| Tropic of Capricorn | 23½° S |
| Arctic Circle | 66½° N |
| Antarctic Circle | 66½° S |
| North Pole / South Pole | 90° N / 90° S |

- **Heat zones:** **Torrid** (hottest, between the two tropics), **Temperate** (between the tropics and the polar circles), **Frigid** (coldest, beyond the polar circles)

### Longitudes (meridians)

- Imaginary **half-circles** that join the **North and South Poles**. All longitudes are **of equal length**.
- The **Prime Meridian (0°)** passes through **Greenwich, near London**. It divides the Earth into the **Eastern and Western Hemispheres**.
- There are **360 longitudes**: 180° E and 180° W. The 180° line is roughly the **International Date Line**.
- **Time:** the Earth turns 360° in 24 hours, which is **15° in 1 hour**, or **1° in 4 minutes**.
- **Indian Standard Time (IST)** is based on **82½° E** (near Mirzapur, U.P.). IST = GMT **+ 5 hours 30 minutes**.

### Grid

- The network of latitudes and longitudes is called the **grid**. It helps to find the **exact location** of any place.

:::trick
**LATitude = LADDER** (horizontal rungs, going up and down). **LONGitude = LONG lines from pole to pole.**
**"Cancer is up north, Capricorn is down south"** (C-up / C-down: "Cancer Climbs, Capricorn Comes down").
:::

:::mumbai
Mumbai is at about **19° N, 72.8° E**. It lies **south of the Tropic of Cancer**, so it is in the **Torrid Zone** and stays warm all year!
:::

## 2. Maps

- A **map** is a drawing of the Earth's surface, or a part of it, on a **flat surface**, drawn to **scale**.
- **Map vs globe:** a map is easy to carry and can show small areas in detail, but it **distorts** shapes and sizes a little.
- **Cartography** is the art of making maps. A **cartographer** is a map-maker. An **atlas** is a book of maps.

### Types of maps

| Type | Shows | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical (relief)** | Natural features: mountains, rivers, plains | A physical map of India |
| **Political** | Boundaries of countries and states, cities | A map of Indian states |
| **Thematic** | One particular theme | Maps of rainfall, crops, population |

### Components of a map

1. **Title:** what the map is about
2. **Direction:** the north arrow (N). The **cardinal directions** are N, S, E and W, and the **intermediate directions** are NE, NW, SE and SW.
3. **Scale:** the ratio between a distance on the map and on the ground
   - Statement scale: "1 cm = 10 km"
   - Representative fraction (RF): 1 : 1,00,000
   - Linear (graphic) scale: a line divided into parts
4. **Legend (key):** explains the symbols and colours used
5. **Conventional signs and symbols:** the same everywhere (a railway line, a temple, a post office)

### Colours on a physical map

| Colour | Shows |
|---|---|
| Blue | Water bodies |
| Green | Lowlands, plains |
| Yellow and brown | Highlands, plateaus, mountains |
| White | Snow-covered peaks |

- **Sketch:** drawn **without scale**, from memory. **Plan:** a **large-scale** drawing of a small area (a house or a school).

:::trick
**Map components: "TDSLC = Ten Dancing Snakes Love Chocolate"**: **T**itle, **D**irection, **S**cale, **L**egend, **C**onventional signs.
**Directions: "Never Eat Soggy Waffles"** (clockwise from N: North, East, South, West).
:::

## 3. Landforms: Mountains, Plateaus and Plains

- **Landforms** are the natural features of the Earth's surface. The three main ones are **mountains, plateaus and plains**.

### Mountains

- A **mountain** is a landform that rises **steeply** from the surrounding land, with a **peak (summit)**. It is generally higher than about **600 m**.
- A **range** is a line of mountains. A **valley** is the low land between mountains.

| Type | How it formed | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Fold mountains** | Rock layers were **pushed and folded** by forces inside the Earth. They are young and high. | **Himalayas**, Alps, **Andes**, **Rockies** |
| **Block mountains** | Land **lifted up** between cracks (faults). The sunken part forms a **rift valley**. | Vosges and Black Forest (Europe), **Satpura** (India) |
| **Volcanic mountains** | Lava piled up around an opening | Mt Fuji (Japan), Mt **Kilimanjaro** (Africa), Vesuvius (Italy) |
| **Residual mountains** | Old mountains **worn down** by erosion | **Aravallis**, Nilgiris (India) |

- **Rift valley:** a long, narrow valley formed when land sinks between two faults: the **Great Rift Valley** (Africa), the **Narmada valley** (India).
- **Life in the mountains:** a cold climate, thin air, little farming (**terrace farming** on slopes), tourism, few roads.

### Plateaus

- A **plateau** is a **raised, flat-topped** land with steep sides. It is called a "**table-land**".

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Intermontane** (surrounded by mountains) | **Tibetan Plateau**, the **highest** plateau ("Roof of the World") |
| **Piedmont** (at the foot of mountains) | **Patagonia** (South America), Malwa (India) |
| **Continental** (rises from the plains or sea, a large area) | **Deccan Plateau** (India) |

- Plateaus are **rich in minerals** (Chota Nagpur is the "mineral heartland of India") and have **waterfalls** (Jog Falls, Hundru Falls).

### Plains

- **Plains** are **flat, low-lying** lands, usually less than 200 m high.
- **Depositional (alluvial) plains** are formed by rivers depositing silt: the **Indo-Gangetic Plain**, the Mississippi Plain.
- **Plains are the most populated areas**: fertile soil, easy farming, easy to build roads, railways and cities.

:::trick
**Mountain types: "FBVR = Five Big Volcanic Rocks"**: **F**old, **B**lock, **V**olcanic, **R**esidual.
**Mountain = an ice-cream cone (peak). Plateau = a table (flat top). Plain = the floor (flat and low).**
:::

:::

## 4. Water Bodies

- About **71%** of the Earth is covered by water. That's why the Earth is called the **Blue Planet**.

### Oceans

| Ocean | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Pacific** | The **largest and deepest**. The **Mariana Trench** (about 11,000 m) is the deepest point on Earth. |
| **Atlantic** | The second largest, **S-shaped**, with the busiest shipping routes |
| **Indian** | The only ocean **named after a country** |
| **Southern (Antarctic)** | Surrounds Antarctica |
| **Arctic** | The **smallest and shallowest**, frozen for most of the year |

### Seas, bays and gulfs

- A **sea** is a part of an ocean, partly enclosed by land.
- **Marginal sea:** on the edge of an ocean (the **Arabian Sea**, South China Sea).
- **Inland or enclosed sea:** almost surrounded by land (the **Mediterranean Sea**, Black Sea).
- **Bay:** a wide curved inlet (the **Bay of Bengal**). **Gulf:** a deep, narrow inlet (the Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Mexico).
- **Strait:** a narrow water passage joining two seas (the **Palk Strait**). **Isthmus:** a narrow strip of land joining two land masses (the **Isthmus of Panama**).

### Lakes

| Record | Lake |
|---|---|
| Largest lake (saltwater) | **Caspian Sea** |
| Largest freshwater lake | **Lake Superior** (North America) |
| Deepest lake | **Lake Baikal** (Russia) |
| Highest navigable lake | **Lake Titicaca** (South America) |
| Largest lagoon in India | **Chilika** (Odisha) |

### Rivers

- **Source:** where the river begins. **Mouth:** where it meets the sea.
- **Tributary:** a smaller river that **joins** the main river. **Distributary:** a branch that **leaves** the main river near the mouth.
- **Delta:** a triangular area of deposited silt at the mouth (the **Sundarbans delta**, the Ganga–Brahmaputra). **Estuary:** a wide, deep mouth with no delta (the **Narmada and Tapi**).
- **Meander:** a loop in a river. An **oxbow lake** is a cut-off meander.
- **Longest river:** the **Nile** (Africa). **Largest by volume:** the **Amazon** (South America).

:::trick
**The 5 oceans by size: "Please Adjust Indian Shoes Accordingly"**: **P**acific, **A**tlantic, **I**ndian, **S**outhern, **A**rctic.
**Tributary = joins in ("tribute" = gives water). Distributary = distributes (leaves).**
:::

:::mumbai
Mumbai is on the **Arabian Sea** (a marginal sea of the Indian Ocean). It was once **seven islands** that were joined together! The **Mithi River** flows through the city and enters the sea at **Mahim Creek**. **Powai, Vihar and Tulsi** are lakes inside Mumbai.
:::

## 5. Agriculture

- **Agriculture** is growing **crops** and rearing **animals**. More than **half of India's** workers depend on it.
- **Factors affecting agriculture:** climate (temperature, rainfall), soil, relief (flat land), water (irrigation), and human factors (labour, machines, markets).

### Types of agriculture

| Type | Features | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subsistence** | Grown **for the family's own use**, on small farms with simple tools | Small Indian villages |
| **Commercial** | Grown **for sale**, on large farms with machines | Wheat farms in the USA and Canada |
| **Plantation** | **A single cash crop** on a big estate | Tea in Assam, coffee in Karnataka, rubber in Kerala |
| **Shifting (slash and burn)** | Clear the forest, farm, then **move on** when the soil loses its fertility | **Jhum** (North-East India), the Amazon basin |
| **Mixed farming** | Crops + animal rearing together | Europe, Punjab |
| **Intensive** | Small land, a lot of labour, high output per acre | Rice in India, China |
| **Extensive** | Large land, machines, less labour | The Prairies (North America) |

### Crop seasons of India

| Season | Sown | Harvested | Crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kharif** | June–July (monsoon) | Sept–Oct | **Rice**, cotton, maize, jowar, bajra, soybean |
| **Rabi** | Oct–Dec (winter) | March–April | **Wheat**, mustard, gram, barley |
| **Zaid** | March–June (summer) | June | Watermelon, cucumber, vegetables |

### Major crops

| Crop | Conditions | Major areas |
|---|---|---|
| **Rice** | High temperature (above 25 °C), **heavy rain (100–200 cm)**, clayey soil, standing water | West Bengal, U.P., Punjab, **Konkan**; China, Bangladesh |
| **Wheat** | Cool, moist growing season, **moderate rain (50–75 cm)**, a sunny ripening period, loamy soil | Punjab, Haryana, U.P., M.P.; the USA and Canada Prairies |
| **Cotton** | Warm, frost-free, moderate rain, **black soil (regur)** | **Maharashtra (Vidarbha)**, Gujarat; the USA |
| **Sugarcane** | Hot and humid, plenty of water | U.P., **Maharashtra (Kolhapur, Sangli)**; Brazil |
| **Tea** | Warm, heavy rain, **well-drained hill slopes** | Assam, Darjeeling; Sri Lanka, China |
| **Coffee** | Warm, shady hill slopes | Karnataka; **Brazil** (the largest producer) |

- **Food crops:** rice, wheat, millets, pulses. **Cash crops:** cotton, jute, sugarcane, tea, coffee.
- **Green Revolution (1960s):** high-yielding seeds, fertilisers and irrigation greatly increased **wheat and rice** production in India.

:::trick
**"KR-Z = Kharif Rain, Rabi wintER, Zaid summer"**
**"Rice loves Rain, Wheat loves Winter."**
:::

## 6. Minerals

- A **mineral** is a **natural substance** with a definite chemical composition, found in the Earth's crust. **Ores** are rocks from which minerals are taken out profitably.

### Classification

| Metallic | | Non-metallic | Fuel (energy) minerals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ferrous** (contain iron) | **Non-ferrous** (no iron) | | |
| Iron ore, manganese | Copper, bauxite (aluminium), gold, silver | Mica, limestone, gypsum, salt | **Coal, petroleum, natural gas** (fossil fuels) |

### Important minerals and their uses

| Mineral | Uses | Where in India |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron ore** | Steel making (buildings, machines) | Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Goa |
| **Bauxite** | **Aluminium** (aircraft, utensils, foil) | Odisha, Gujarat, Jharkhand |
| **Copper** | Electric wires (it is a good conductor) | Rajasthan (Khetri), Jharkhand |
| **Mica** | Electrical insulation | Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh |
| **Limestone** | Cement | M.P., Rajasthan |
| **Coal** | Fuel for thermal power and the iron and steel industry | Jharkhand (Jharia), Odisha, Chhattisgarh |
| **Petroleum** | Fuel (petrol, diesel), plastics | **Mumbai High** (offshore), Assam (Digboi), Gujarat |

- **Mining methods:** open-cast (surface), shaft (deep underground), drilling (oil and gas)
- **Conservation:** minerals are **non-renewable**, so reduce, reuse and recycle (recycle aluminium cans and scrap iron) and use substitutes.

:::trick
**Ferrous = Fe = iron.** "If it has **Fe**, it's **Fe**rrous!"
**Bauxite → Aluminium: "B for Bauxite, B for Bottles and Bikes made of aluminium."**
:::

:::mumbai
**Mumbai High** is an offshore oil field in the Arabian Sea, about **160 km off the Mumbai coast**, discovered in **1974**. It is one of India's most important oil fields!
:::

:::

## 7. North America

### Location and size

- The **3rd largest continent**. It lies entirely in the **Northern and Western Hemispheres**.
- **Boundaries:** the **Arctic Ocean** (north), the **Atlantic Ocean** (east), the **Pacific Ocean** (west), and the **Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean Sea** (south-east).
- It is joined to South America by the **Isthmus of Panama**. The **Panama Canal** connects the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans.
- **Greenland**, the **largest island** in the world, is a part of North America.

### Political divisions (learn these from the atlas)

- **Canada** (the 2nd largest country in the world), the **USA**, **Mexico**, the Central American countries and the Caribbean islands (Cuba, Jamaica…)

### Physical divisions

| Region | Features |
|---|---|
| **Western Cordillera** | Young fold mountains: the **Rockies**. The highest peak is **Denali (Mt McKinley)**. Plateaus such as Colorado, with the **Grand Canyon**. **Death Valley** is the lowest and hottest place. |
| **Great Central Plains** | Flat, fertile plains: the **Prairies** (grasslands, the "**bread basket of the world**", famous for **wheat**) |
| **Eastern Highlands** | Old, worn-down mountains: the **Appalachians** |
| **Canadian (Laurentian) Shield** | Ancient hard rocks around **Hudson Bay**, with many lakes and rich minerals |

### Rivers and lakes

- **Mississippi–Missouri:** the longest river system of North America. It flows into the **Gulf of Mexico**.
- **St. Lawrence** (drains the Great Lakes into the Atlantic), **Mackenzie** (flows into the Arctic), **Colorado** (formed the Grand Canyon), **Yukon**, **Rio Grande** (the USA–Mexico border)
- **The Great Lakes:** **Superior** (the largest freshwater lake in the world), **Michigan**, **Huron**, **Erie**, **Ontario**. **Niagara Falls** lies between Lake Erie and Lake Ontario.

### Climate and vegetation

- The far north is **tundra** (very cold: mosses, lichens, the **Inuit** people). Canada has **coniferous forests** (softwood trees). The south is warmer: grasslands and deserts.

### Lumbering in Canada (case study)

- Canada has huge **coniferous forests** of **pine, fir and spruce** with **soft wood**, which is good for **paper, pulp and furniture**.
- **Why lumbering is easy:** pure stands of trees (one kind together), frozen ground in winter for moving logs, rivers to **float logs** down to the mills in spring, cheap hydroelectric power and machines.
- **Conservation:** afforestation (planting new trees) and controlled cutting.

:::trick
**The Great Lakes: "HOMES"**: **H**uron, **O**ntario, **M**ichigan, **E**rie, **S**uperior.
**From west to east: "Rockies → Prairies → Appalachians" = "Young → Flat → Old"**.
:::

:::

## 8. South America

### Location and size

- The **4th largest continent**. The **Equator** passes through its northern part (**Ecuador, Brazil**), and the **Tropic of Capricorn** crosses it further south. So it lies in both hemispheres, mostly in the **Southern** one.
- **Boundaries:** the **Caribbean Sea** (north), the **Atlantic Ocean** (east), the **Pacific Ocean** (west). The southern tip is **Cape Horn**, by the **Drake Passage**.

### Political divisions (learn these from the atlas)

- **Brazil** (the largest country), Argentina, Peru, Colombia, Chile, Venezuela, Bolivia, Ecuador, Paraguay, Uruguay, Guyana, Suriname

### Physical divisions

| Region | Features |
|---|---|
| **The Andes** (west) | The **longest mountain range in the world** (young fold mountains). The highest peak is **Mt Aconcagua** (Argentina). **Lake Titicaca** is on the Peru–Bolivia border. |
| **Central plains** | **Amazon basin** (hot, wet rainforest), the **Llanos** (Orinoco grasslands), the **Pampas** (Argentina, temperate grasslands for wheat and cattle) |
| **Eastern Highlands** | **Brazilian Highlands** and **Guiana Highlands** (where **Angel Falls**, the **highest waterfall in the world**, is in Venezuela) |
| **Patagonia** | A dry plateau in southern Argentina |
| **Atacama Desert** | In northern Chile, **one of the driest places on Earth** |

### Rivers

- **Amazon:** the **largest river in the world by volume**. It flows west to east into the Atlantic and has more than 1,000 tributaries.
- **Orinoco** (Venezuela), **Paraná–Paraguay** (flows south into the **Rio de la Plata**)

### Life in the Amazon basin (case study)

- **Climate:** **hot and wet all year** (equatorial), with rain almost every afternoon.
- **Vegetation:** **dense tropical rainforests** called **selvas**: tall evergreen trees, the "**lungs of the Earth**".
- **Animals:** jaguar, anaconda, **piranha**, sloth, toucan, macaw, tree frogs.
- **People:** tribes who hunt, fish, gather and do **shifting cultivation** (they grow cassava). Their houses have sloping roofs, sometimes on stilts. They travel by **boat** on the rivers.
- **Problems:** **deforestation** for farming, cattle ranching, roads and mining. It threatens wildlife, tribal life and the world's climate.

### Grasslands of South America

| Name | Country |
|---|---|
| Llanos | Venezuela and Colombia |
| Campos | Brazil |
| Pampas | Argentina |

:::trick
**"Amazon = Amazing volume. Andes = Always long."**
**Grasslands: "Lazy Cows Pamper themselves"**: **L**lanos, **C**ampos, **P**ampas (from north to south).
:::

:::mumbai
Brazil is the world's **largest coffee producer**, so there's a good chance some of the coffee in Mumbai's cafés has travelled from Brazil! Also, the Amazon rainforest and Mumbai's **Sanjay Gandhi National Park** are both green "lungs", just of very different sizes.
:::
