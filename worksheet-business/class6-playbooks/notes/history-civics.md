### BC/BCE and AD/CE

- **BC (BCE)** means before the birth of Christ. Count **backwards**: 500 BC is **older** than 200 BC.
- **AD (CE)** means after the birth of Christ. Count **forwards**.
- **Century:** 100 years. The 3rd century BC = 300–201 BC.

## 1. Introduction to History

- **History** is the study of the **past**, based on **evidence** or **sources**.
- **Pre-history:** the time before writing was invented. **Proto-history:** writing existed but can't be read yet (the Harappan script). **History:** the period with written records we can read.

### Sources of history

| Archaeological sources | Literary sources |
|---|---|
| **Inscriptions** (writing on rocks, pillars, copper plates) | **Religious texts**: the Vedas, Jataka tales, Jain Agamas |
| **Coins** (tell us about kings, dates and trade) | **Secular texts**: Kautilya's *Arthashastra*, Kalidasa's plays |
| **Monuments** (temples, stupas, forts) | **Foreign accounts**: Megasthenes (*Indica*), Fa-Hien, Hiuen Tsang |
| **Artefacts** (pots, tools, jewellery, seals) | |

- **Archaeologists** dig up and study remains. **Historians** study sources to write history.
- **Carbon dating** helps to find out the age of old objects.

:::trick
**Sources = "MICA"**: **M**onuments, **I**nscriptions, **C**oins, **A**rtefacts (archaeological) + books (literary).
:::

:::mumbai
**Kanheri Caves** in Sanjay Gandhi National Park, Borivali, are more than 2,000 years old. Their **inscriptions** are a real historical source right inside Mumbai!
:::

## 2. Early Man

- Early humans were **hunter-gatherers**. They moved from place to place (**nomadic**) in search of food.

| Age | Features |
|---|---|
| **Palaeolithic (Old Stone Age)** | Rough stone tools, hunting and gathering, lived in caves, **discovered fire** |
| **Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age)** | Small, sharp tools (**microliths**), began to **tame animals** (the dog was the first) |
| **Neolithic (New Stone Age)** | **Polished** stone tools, **farming** began, **settled life** in villages, the **wheel** was invented, pottery and weaving |
| **Chalcolithic (Copper-Stone Age)** | Copper tools used alongside stone ones |

- **Bhimbetka** (Madhya Pradesh): rock shelters with cave paintings
- **Mehrgarh** (now in Pakistan): one of the earliest farming villages

### Important discoveries

1. **Fire:** warmth, cooking, protection from animals, light
2. **Wheel:** transport (carts) and pottery (the potter's wheel)
3. **Farming:** a settled life, villages, surplus food

:::trick
**"Paleo → Meso → Neo" = "Old → Middle → New"** (like Junior, Middle and Senior school!)
**Neolithic = Never moving again** (they settled down and farmed).
:::

:::play Stone Age Charades
Students act out early humans: hunting, making fire, painting cave walls, taming a dog, farming, using a wheel. The class guesses the activity **and** the age!
:::

:::check
1. Why were early humans nomadic?
2. What is a microlith?
3. Name two great discoveries of the Neolithic Age.
4. Where are the Bhimbetka rock shelters?
:::

## 3. The Indus Valley (Harappan) Civilisation

- **Period:** about **2500 BC to 1750 BC**. It developed along the **Indus river** and its tributaries.
- **Discovered** in **1921** by **Daya Ram Sahni** (Harappa). **R.D. Banerji** discovered **Mohenjo-daro** in **1922**. **Sir John Marshall** led the excavations.
- **Important sites:** Harappa and Mohenjo-daro (Pakistan); **Lothal** and **Dholavira** (Gujarat); **Kalibangan** (Rajasthan); **Rakhigarhi** and Banawali (Haryana); Ropar (Punjab)

### Town planning (the most remarkable feature)

- Cities were planned on a **grid pattern**: streets met at **right angles**.
- They were divided into a **citadel** (a raised upper town for important buildings) and a **lower town** (where people lived).
- **Covered drains** ran along the streets. It was one of the **best drainage systems** of the ancient world.
- Houses were made of **burnt bricks** of standard sizes, with wells and bathrooms.
- **The Great Bath** at Mohenjo-daro was waterproofed with bitumen.
- **The Granary** stored grain, and the **dockyard at Lothal** shows sea trade.

### Life of the people

- **Occupations:** farming (wheat, barley, cotton, the first people to grow cotton), trade (with Mesopotamia), crafts (beads, pottery, seals)
- **Seals:** made of **steatite**, with animals such as the **unicorn**, bull and elephant, and a script that **has not yet been read**
- **Religion:** worship of the **Mother Goddess**, a "**Pashupati**" seal (a proto-Shiva figure), trees (peepal) and animals
- **Art:** the **Dancing Girl** (bronze) and the **Priest King** (stone statue)

### Decline (possible reasons)

Floods, earthquakes, changes in the river's course, drying of the Saraswati river, invasion, epidemics

:::trick
**Harappan sites: "Hari Mohan Lost Doll Kali Rakhi"**: Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Lothal, Dholavira, Kalibangan, Rakhigarhi.
**The Harappans were "Grid Gurus"**: grid streets, great drains, the Great Bath, granary.
:::

:::mumbai
Mumbai still struggles with drainage every monsoon. But the Harappans had **covered drains 4,500 years ago**! Ask students: "What can Mumbai learn from Mohenjo-daro?"
:::

:::play Plan a Harappan City
On chart paper, teams design a city with a citadel, a lower town, grid roads, covered drains, a Great Bath and a granary. Each team explains its plan like a city architect.
:::

:::check
1. When and by whom was Harappa discovered?
2. Describe three features of Harappan town planning.
3. What were Harappan seals made of? Name one animal on them.
4. Name the dockyard site of the Harappan civilisation.

## 4. Mesopotamian, Egyptian and Chinese Civilisations

| | **Mesopotamian** | **Egyptian** | **Chinese** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **River** | **Tigris and Euphrates** ("land between two rivers") | **Nile** ("Egypt is the gift of the Nile") | **Hwang Ho (Yellow River)** |
| **Present country** | Iraq | Egypt | China |
| **Writing** | **Cuneiform** (wedge-shaped, on clay tablets) | **Hieroglyphics** (picture writing, on **papyrus**) | Pictographic script (ideograms) |
| **Famous for** | **Ziggurats** (temple towers), the **wheel**, the 60-minute hour and 360° circle, the **Code of Hammurabi** (laws) | **Pyramids** (tombs of the **Pharaohs**), **mummies**, a 365-day calendar, the Sphinx | **Silk**, paper, the compass, the **Great Wall**, oracle bones |
| **Rulers** | City-state kings (Sumerians, Babylonians) | **Pharaohs** (god-kings) | Dynasties (Shang) |

- Hammurabi's law code included the idea of "**an eye for an eye**".
- The **Great Pyramid of Giza** was built for Pharaoh **Khufu**.
- The **Hwang Ho** is called "**China's Sorrow**" because of its floods.

:::trick
**Rivers: "TE-N-H"**: Tigris and Euphrates = Mesopotamia, Nile = Egypt, Hwang Ho = China.
**Writing: "Clay-Cuneiform, Papyrus-Pictures (hieroglyphics)"**
:::

:::

:::check
1. Which rivers is Mesopotamia located between?
2. Why is Egypt called "the gift of the Nile"?
3. What is a ziggurat?
4. Why is the Hwang Ho called "China's Sorrow"?

## 5. The Vedic Civilisation

- The **Aryans** came to the **Sapta Sindhu** region (the land of seven rivers, in the north-west of India) around **1500 BC**.
- **Early Vedic Period** (about 1500–1000 BC) and **Later Vedic Period** (about 1000–600 BC)

### Vedic literature

- **Four Vedas:** **Rig Veda** (the oldest: hymns), **Sama Veda** (music and songs), **Yajur Veda** (rituals and sacrifices), **Atharva Veda** (medicine, magic and daily life)
- **Upanishads** (philosophy), **Brahmanas**, **Aranyakas**, and the epics, the **Ramayana** (by Valmiki) and the **Mahabharata** (by Ved Vyasa)

### Life in the Vedic Age

| | Early Vedic | Later Vedic |
|---|---|---|
| **Society** | The **family** was the basic unit, women had **respect** (Gargi, Maitreyi, Apala), no strict caste | **The Varna system became rigid**: Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, Shudras. Women's status fell. |
| **Polity** | Tribal: a **Rajan** (chief) with the **Sabha** (council of elders) and the **Samiti** (assembly of the people) | Bigger kingdoms (**Janapadas**), the king became powerful, big sacrifices (**Ashvamedha**, Rajasuya) |
| **Economy** | Cattle-rearing (cows were wealth) | **Farming** with iron ploughs |
| **Religion** | Nature gods: **Indra** (rain), **Agni** (fire), **Varuna** (water), **Surya** | Prajapati, Vishnu, Rudra; rituals became complex |

- **The four ashramas (stages of life):** Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (retired), Sanyasa (renunciation)
- **Education:** in **gurukuls**, where students lived with the teacher

:::trick
**Vedas: "Rita Sang Yummy Atchaar"**: Rig, Sama, Yajur, Atharva.
**Ashramas: "Students Get Very Sleepy"**: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha, Vanaprastha, Sanyasa.
:::

:::play Sabha vs Samiti Role-Play
Split the class into a Sabha (elders) and a Samiti (people's assembly). They debate a village problem ("Should we move to a new grazing land?"). The Rajan (a student) decides. This shows early democracy!
:::

:::check
1. Name the four Vedas. Which is the oldest?
2. What were the Sabha and the Samiti?
3. Name two learned women of the Vedic Age.
4. Write the four ashramas in order.
:::

## 6. Jainism and Buddhism

- In the **6th century BC**, people questioned costly rituals and the rigid caste system. Two great teachers showed a simple path.

| | **Jainism** | **Buddhism** |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder or great teacher** | **Vardhamana Mahavira**, the **24th Tirthankara** (the 1st was Rishabhanatha) | **Gautama Buddha** (Siddhartha) |
| **Born** | **Kundagrama** near Vaishali (Bihar), 540 BC | **Lumbini** (Nepal), 563 BC |
| **Enlightenment** | **Kaivalya** at the age of 42, becoming a **Jina** (conqueror) | Under the **Bodhi tree** at **Bodh Gaya** |
| **First sermon** | | **Sarnath**, near Varanasi |
| **Died** | **Pavapuri** (Bihar) | **Kushinagar** (U.P.), called the **Mahaparinirvana** |
| **Main teachings** | **Three Jewels (Triratna):** right faith, right knowledge, right conduct. **Five vows:** **ahimsa** (non-violence), truth, non-stealing, non-possession, chastity | **Four Noble Truths**, the **Eightfold Path**, the **Middle Path** (no extreme luxury and no extreme suffering) |
| **Sects** | **Digambaras** (sky-clad) and **Svetambaras** (white-clad) | **Hinayana** and **Mahayana** |
| **Holy books** | **Agamas** | **Tripitakas** (written in **Pali**) |

### The Four Noble Truths (Buddha)

1. Life is full of **suffering**.
2. **Desire** is the cause of suffering.
3. Suffering ends when desire ends.
4. Follow the **Eightfold Path** to end desire.

:::trick
**Buddha's 4 holy places: "Lumbini–Bodh Gaya–Sarnath–Kushinagar" = "Born–Became Buddha–Spoke–Said goodbye"**.
**Jainism = "Jeeva-daya"**: be kind to every living being (ahimsa).
:::

:::mumbai
Mumbai has Buddhist history! The **Kanheri Caves** (Borivali) were a big Buddhist monastery with more than 100 caves. The **Global Vipassana Pagoda** at Gorai is a modern monument to the Buddha's teachings, which students can visit.
:::

:::play Path Game
Draw the Eightfold Path as 8 squares on the floor. A student steps on each square and gives a real-life example ("Right speech: I won't lie to my friend"). The class claps for good examples.
:::

:::check
1. Who was the 24th Tirthankara?
2. Where did the Buddha get enlightenment and give his first sermon?
3. Name the Three Jewels of Jainism.
4. Write the Four Noble Truths.
:::

## 7. The Mauryan Empire

- **Founder:** **Chandragupta Maurya** (321 BC), with the help of **Chanakya (Kautilya)**, who wrote the **Arthashastra** (a book on government).
- **Capital:** **Pataliputra** (now Patna).
- **Megasthenes**, a Greek ambassador sent by Seleucus, lived in Chandragupta's court and wrote **Indica**.
- **Bindusara:** Chandragupta's son.

### Ashoka the Great (273–232 BC)

- He fought the **Kalinga War** (about **261 BC**, in present-day Odisha). He was very sad at the bloodshed (about 1 lakh killed), gave up war and followed **Buddhism**.
- **Dhamma (Dharma):** his code of moral conduct: non-violence, respect for elders, kindness to animals and servants, tolerance of all religions.
- He spread his message through **rock edicts and pillar edicts** in **Prakrit** (in the **Brahmi** script).
- He sent missionaries, including his son **Mahendra** and daughter **Sanghamitra**, to **Sri Lanka**.
- **The Lion Capital of the Sarnath pillar** is now **India's National Emblem**, and the **Ashoka Chakra** is on our **national flag**.

### Mauryan administration

- The **king** was supreme, helped by a council of ministers (**Mantri Parishad**).
- The empire was divided into **provinces**, each under a prince. Spies kept the king informed.
- **Pataliputra** was run by a **committee of 30 members** (6 boards of 5 each).

:::trick
**Mauryan dynasty: "Chandra Bindu Ashok" (C-B-A)**: Chandragupta → Bindusara → Ashoka.
**Ashoka's change: "War → Wow → Peace"** (Kalinga war → shock and regret → Dhamma).
:::

:::mumbai
Fragments of an **Ashokan rock edict** were found at **Sopara (Nala Sopara)**, just north of Mumbai! In ancient times Sopara (Shurparaka) was a busy **port**. Ashoka's message reached our own city's doorstep!
:::

:::play Ashoka's Edict Writing
Each student writes their own "edict" (a rule for a kind and peaceful classroom) on a "stone" made of grey chart paper. Display them as the "Class Edict Pillar". Discuss: how are these like Ashoka's Dhamma?
:::

:::check
1. Who founded the Mauryan Empire? Who helped him?
2. What was the result of the Kalinga War?
3. What is Dhamma?
4. Which Mauryan monument is now our National Emblem?
:::

## 8. The Sangam Age

- **Sangam** means an **assembly** of Tamil **poets and scholars**, held at **Madurai** under the patronage of the **Pandya** kings.
- **Period:** about **300 BC to AD 300**, in the far south of India (**Tamilakam**).
- **Three Sangams** were held. The literature of the third Sangam survives.

### The three kingdoms

| Kingdom | Capital | Emblem | Famous ruler |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cheras** | Vanji (Kerala) | **Bow** | Senguttuvan |
| **Cholas** | Uraiyur (and the port of **Puhar/Kaveripattinam**) | **Tiger** | **Karikala** (built the **Grand Anicut (Kallanai)** dam on the Kaveri) |
| **Pandyas** | **Madurai** | **Fish** | Nedunjeliyan |

### Sangam literature and life

- **Tolkappiyam:** a book of Tamil grammar
- **Silappadikaram** (story of an anklet) and **Manimekalai**: the two great epics
- **Tirukkural** by **Thiruvalluvar**: wise sayings
- **Trade:** busy sea trade with the **Romans** in pepper, pearls, ivory, spices and muslin. Roman coins have been found in the south. The ports included **Muziris**, Puhar and Korkai.

:::trick
**Emblems: "Chera's Bow, Chola's Tiger, Pandya's Fish": "Bow-Tiger-Fish" (B-T-F)**.
**Sangam = "Sammelan" of poets.**
:::

:::play Kingdom Flags
Three teams are the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas. Each makes a flag with its emblem and presents its capital, ruler and one achievement. Then hold a "Sangam" where each team recites a 2-line poem!
:::

:::check
1. What does "Sangam" mean? Where was it held?
2. Name the emblems of the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas.
3. Who wrote the Tirukkural?
4. Which Chola king built the Grand Anicut?
:::

## 9. The Age of the Guptas: the Golden Age

- **Founder:** **Sri Gupta**. **Chandragupta I** (AD 320) started the Gupta era and took the title **Maharajadhiraja**.
- **Samudragupta:** a great conqueror. He is called the "**Napoleon of India**" (by historian V.A. Smith). He was also a poet and **veena** player. The **Allahabad Pillar Inscription**, by his court poet **Harisena**, describes his conquests.
- **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya):** defeated the **Shakas**. His court had the **Navaratnas** (nine gems), including **Kalidasa**. The Chinese traveller **Fa-Hien** visited India in his reign.

### Why is it called the Golden Age?

| Field | Achievements |
|---|---|
| **Literature** | **Kalidasa**: *Abhijnana Shakuntalam*, *Meghaduta*, *Raghuvamsha*. **Vishnu Sharma**: the *Panchatantra* |
| **Science and maths** | **Aryabhata**: the concept of **zero**, the value of **pi (π)**, said that the **Earth rotates on its axis**. **Varahamihira**: astronomy. **Brahmagupta**: mathematics |
| **Medicine** | **Sushruta** (surgery), **Charaka** (medicine). (Their works were compiled and studied widely in this period.) |
| **Art and architecture** | **Ajanta cave paintings** (some caves), the **Dashavatara temple** at Deogarh, the **Iron Pillar** at Mehrauli, Delhi, which **has not rusted** in 1,600 years! |
| **Education** | **Nalanda University** was founded |
| **Coins** | Beautiful **gold coins** (dinaras) |

:::trick
**The Gupta kings: "Chandu Samosa Chandu"**: Chandragupta I → Samudragupta → Chandragupta II.
**Golden Age "LSAE"**: **L**iterature, **S**cience, **A**rt, **E**ducation, all shining like gold!
:::

:::mumbai
The world-famous **Ajanta Caves** (Aurangabad district, Maharashtra) have paintings from different periods, some from the Gupta age. They are a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** in our own state. Plan a class trip!
:::

:::play Navaratna Quiz Show
Nine students each play a "gem" (Kalidasa, Aryabhata, Varahamihira…). The class asks yes/no questions to guess who is who. The best "gem" performance wins!
:::

:::check
1. Why is Samudragupta called the "Napoleon of India"?
2. Name two works of Kalidasa.
3. What were Aryabhata's contributions?
4. Give two reasons why the Gupta age is called the Golden Age.
:::

## 10. Civics: Rural Local Self-Government (Panchayati Raj)

- **Local self-government:** local people elect their own representatives to solve **local problems** (water, roads, cleanliness, schools).
- The **73rd Constitutional Amendment (1992)** gave Panchayati Raj **constitutional status**.
- It is a **three-tier system**:

| Level | Body | Head |
|---|---|---|
| **Village** | **Gram Panchayat** | **Sarpanch** (with an Up-Sarpanch) |
| **Block** | **Panchayat Samiti** | **Chairman (Sabhapati)** |
| **District** | **Zilla Parishad** | **Chairman (Adhyaksha)** |

### Gram Sabha and Gram Panchayat

- **Gram Sabha:** **all adults (18+)** in the village who are on the voters' list. They elect the Gram Panchayat and check its work.
- **Gram Panchayat:** elected members (**Panchs**) and the **Sarpanch**, for **5 years**.
- **Reservation:** seats are reserved for **SCs, STs** and **women (at least one-third)**. In Maharashtra, **50%** of seats are reserved for women.
- **The Secretary (Gram Sevak)** keeps records.

### Functions of the Gram Panchayat

- **Compulsory:** clean drinking water, village roads, drainage and sanitation, street lighting, primary schools, recording births and deaths
- **Optional:** libraries, playgrounds, community halls, planting trees
- **Income:** taxes (on houses, land and markets), fees, and **government grants**

### Nyaya Panchayat

- Settles **small disputes** in villages quickly and cheaply.

:::trick
**Three tiers: "Gram → Samiti → Zilla" = "Village → Block → District"**, like a family: **child → parent → grandparent**.
**Sarpanch = "Sar" (head) + "Panch" (council member).**
:::

:::play Mock Gram Sabha
The class becomes a village. Hold an election for the Sarpanch and 5 Panchs. Then hold a meeting on a problem ("The village well is dirty"). Members propose solutions and vote. Discuss where the money will come from!
:::

:::check
1. What is local self-government?
2. Name the three tiers of Panchayati Raj.
3. What is a Gram Sabha?
4. Write three functions of a Gram Panchayat.
:::

## 11. Civics: Urban Local Self-Government

- The **74th Constitutional Amendment (1992)** gave urban bodies **constitutional status**.

| Body | For | Head |
|---|---|---|
| **Municipal Corporation** | **Big cities** (Mumbai, Pune, Delhi) | **Mayor** (elected), with the **Municipal Commissioner** (an IAS officer) as the administrative head |
| **Municipal Council (Municipality)** | **Smaller towns** | **President (Chairperson)** |
| **Nagar Panchayat** | Areas changing from rural to urban | Chairperson |

### The Municipal Corporation

- The city is divided into **wards**. Each ward elects a **Corporator (Councillor)** for **5 years**.
- The **Mayor** is the first citizen of the city and chairs the meetings. The **Deputy Mayor** helps.
- The **Municipal Commissioner** is appointed by the **state government** and carries out decisions (the executive head).
- **Committees** (such as the Standing Committee) handle finance, health and education.

### Functions

| Compulsory | Optional |
|---|---|
| Water supply, drainage and sewage | Parks, gardens, swimming pools |
| Garbage collection | Libraries, museums |
| Hospitals, vaccination | Housing for the poor |
| Primary schools | City transport |
| Street lights, roads | Markets |
| Fire brigade | Cultural programmes |
| Recording births and deaths | |

- **Income:** **property tax**, water tax, advertisement tax, fees (licences, parking), and grants from the state

:::mumbai
**BMC (Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation)** is one of the **richest municipal corporations in India**. Mumbai is divided into **wards** (A ward, B ward …). The BMC runs the city's **water supply** (from lakes like Vaitarna and Tansa), **BEST buses** (through the BEST Undertaking), **hospitals like KEM and Sion**, municipal schools, **garbage collection**, and the **fire brigade**. Students can find out which ward their home is in and who their Corporator is!
:::

:::trick
**City vs village**: Mayor ↔ Sarpanch, Corporator ↔ Panch, Municipal Corporation ↔ Gram Panchayat.
**"Mayor = Face (the leader you see). Commissioner = Hands (gets the work done)."**
:::

:::play BMC Problem Solvers
Each team picks a real Mumbai problem (potholes, flooding, garbage, traffic). They write a letter to the "Municipal Commissioner" suggesting 3 solutions, and say which department will handle it.
:::

:::check
1. Which body governs a big city? Name its head.
2. What is a ward? Who represents it?
3. Write four compulsory functions of a Municipal Corporation.
4. Name two sources of income of a Municipal Corporation.
:::

## Answer Key

**Ch 1:** (1) The study of the past, based on sources. (2) Archaeological: coins, inscriptions. Literary: the Vedas, the Arthashastra. (3) 300 BC (4) Megasthenes

**Ch 2:** (1) They moved in search of food (hunting and gathering). (2) A small, sharp stone tool of the Mesolithic Age. (3) Farming and the wheel (also pottery) (4) Madhya Pradesh
