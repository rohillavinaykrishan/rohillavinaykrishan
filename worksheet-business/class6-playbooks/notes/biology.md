## 1. The Leaf

- The **leaf** is the **food factory** of the plant. It is green because it contains **chlorophyll**.

### Parts of a leaf

| Part | Description |
|---|---|
| **Leaf base** | Attaches the leaf to the stem |
| **Petiole** | The stalk of the leaf |
| **Lamina (leaf blade)** | The flat green part where food is made |
| **Midrib** | The main central vein |
| **Veins** | Carry water in and food out, and give support |
| **Margin** | The edge of the lamina |
| **Apex** | The tip of the leaf |

- A leaf with a petiole is **petiolate**. Without a petiole it is **sessile**.

### Venation (the pattern of veins)

| Reticulate venation | Parallel venation |
|---|---|
| Veins form a **net** | Veins run **parallel** to each other |
| Found in **dicots** | Found in **monocots** |
| Mango, peepal, hibiscus | Banana, grass, maize, wheat |

### Simple and compound leaves

- **Simple leaf:** one undivided lamina (mango, guava)
- **Compound leaf:** the lamina is divided into **leaflets** (neem, rose, tamarind)

### Functions of the leaf

1. **Photosynthesis**: making food
2. **Transpiration**: losing extra water as vapour through **stomata**
3. **Gas exchange** through stomata
4. **Storage** (onion, aloe vera), **protection** (cactus spines are modified leaves), **climbing** (pea tendrils)

### Photosynthesis

**Carbon dioxide + water —(sunlight, chlorophyll)→ glucose (food) + oxygen**

- **Raw materials:** CO₂ (from the air, through stomata) and water (from the soil, through the roots)
- **Conditions:** **sunlight** and **chlorophyll**
- **Products:** glucose (stored as **starch**) and **oxygen** (released)
- **Starch test:** iodine solution turns starch **blue-black**.

### Stomata

- Tiny pores, mostly on the **lower surface** of the leaf, each surrounded by two **guard cells** that open and close it.

:::trick
**Photosynthesis = "Photo" (light) + "synthesis" (making)**: **making food with light.**
**Leaf parts from bottom to top: "Bina Pet Lamba Mota Aam"**: Base, Petiole, Lamina, Midrib, Apex.
:::

:::mumbai
Sanjay Gandhi National Park in Borivali, and the mangroves along Mumbai's coast, produce huge amounts of oxygen by photosynthesis. They are called the **"lungs of Mumbai"**!
:::

:::play Leaf Detective Walk
Students collect 5 fallen leaves from the school garden. They classify each one: simple or compound? reticulate or parallel? petiolate or sessile? Then they make a leaf chart with labels.
:::

:::check

2. Write the word equation for photosynthesis.
3. Give two examples each of reticulate and parallel venation.
4. What are stomata? Name the cells that surround them.
:::

## 2. The Flower

- The **flower** is the **reproductive part** of the plant. It develops into **fruits and seeds**.

### Parts of a flower (from outside to inside)

| Whorl | Parts | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Calyx** | Sepals (green) | Protects the bud |
| **Corolla** | Petals (colourful) | Attracts insects for pollination |
| **Androecium** (male) | **Stamens**: an **anther** (makes **pollen grains**) + a **filament** (stalk) | Makes pollen |
| **Gynoecium** (female) | **Pistil**: a **stigma** (sticky top) + a **style** (tube) + an **ovary** (contains **ovules**) | Makes ovules, which become seeds |

- **Pedicel:** the stalk of the flower. **Thalamus:** the swollen base the flower parts sit on.
- **Bisexual flower:** has both stamens and a pistil (hibiscus, mustard). **Unisexual flower:** has only one of them (papaya, cucumber, maize).

### Pollination

- **Pollination** is the transfer of **pollen grains** from the **anther** to the **stigma**.
- **Self-pollination:** in the same flower or the same plant. **Cross-pollination:** between different plants of the same kind.
- **Agents:** insects (bees, butterflies), wind, water, birds and animals

### Fertilisation and fruit formation

- The male cell (from pollen) **fuses** with the female cell (in the ovule): this is **fertilisation**.
- After fertilisation: the **ovary → fruit** and the **ovules → seeds**.

:::trick
**From outside to inside: "Can Cats Ask Grandma?"**: **C**alyx, **C**orolla, **A**ndroecium, **G**ynoecium.
**"Anther = Pollen Factory, Stigma = Sticky Landing Pad"**
**Ovary becomes the fruit (O → F). Ovules become the seeds.**
:::

:::mumbai
Mumbai's famous **Alphonso (Hapus) mango** from Ratnagiri grows from a fertilised mango flower. The ovary becomes the juicy fruit, and the ovule becomes the seed (the "guthli")!
:::

:::play Flower Dissection
Bring a hibiscus (shoe flower). Teams carefully remove each whorl and stick them on paper in order: sepals → petals → stamens → pistil. Label each one. Look at the pollen with a hand lens!
:::

:::check
1. Name the four whorls of a flower.
2. What is pollination? Name two agents of pollination.
3. What do the ovary and the ovules become after fertilisation?
4. Give one example each of a bisexual and a unisexual flower.

## 3. The Cell: Structure and Functions

- The **cell** is the **structural and functional unit of life**. All living things are made of cells.
- **Robert Hooke** (1665) first saw cells in a thin slice of **cork** using his microscope.
- **Unicellular** organisms have one cell (amoeba, paramecium, bacteria). **Multicellular** organisms have many cells (humans, plants).
- **Largest cell:** the ostrich egg. **Longest cell in humans:** the nerve cell. **Smallest:** bacteria-like cells (mycoplasma).

### Parts of a cell

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cell membrane** | A thin outer layer that controls what enters and leaves |
| **Cell wall** (plants only) | A rigid outer layer made of **cellulose** that gives shape and protection |
| **Cytoplasm** | A jelly-like fluid where the organelles float |
| **Nucleus** | The **control centre** of the cell, containing the **chromosomes** (DNA) |
| **Mitochondria** | The **powerhouse**, which releases energy from food |
| **Chloroplasts** (plants only) | Contain chlorophyll, where photosynthesis happens |
| **Vacuole** | Stores water and wastes (large in plant cells, small or absent in animal cells) |

### Plant cell vs animal cell

| Plant cell | Animal cell |
|---|---|
| Has a **cell wall** | No cell wall |
| Has **chloroplasts** | No chloroplasts |
| **One large vacuole** | Small vacuoles, or none |
| Usually **rectangular** (fixed shape) | Usually **round or irregular** |

### Levels of organisation

**Cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism**
Example: muscle cells → muscle tissue → heart → circulatory system → human

:::trick
**The cell is a school!**
Nucleus = **Principal** (controls everything). Mitochondria = **Canteen** (gives energy). Cell membrane = **Security guard** (decides who comes in). Cell wall = **Boundary wall**. Cytoplasm = **Playground** where everyone moves around. Vacuole = **Storeroom**.
:::

:::play Build-a-Cell
Each team makes a cell model on a paper plate: green peas for chloroplasts, a cherry or bead for the nucleus, a rubber band for the membrane, a cardboard edge for the cell wall. Is it a plant cell or an animal cell? Other teams have to guess!
:::

:::check
1. Who discovered the cell, and in what?
2. Which part is called (a) the powerhouse (b) the control centre of the cell?
3. Give three differences between plant and animal cells.
4. Write the levels of organisation in order.
:::

## 4. The Digestive System

- **Digestion** is the breakdown of **complex food** into **simple, soluble** substances that the body can absorb.

### The path of food (alimentary canal)

**Mouth → oesophagus (food pipe) → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anus**

| Organ | What happens there |
|---|---|
| **Mouth** | Teeth chew the food. **Saliva** (from the salivary glands) contains an enzyme that starts digesting **starch**. The tongue mixes the food. |
| **Oesophagus** | Food moves down by wave-like muscle movements (**peristalsis**). |
| **Stomach** | **Gastric juice**: **hydrochloric acid** (kills germs) + enzymes that digest **proteins**. |
| **Small intestine** | The **longest part** (about 6–7 m). **Bile** (from the liver) breaks fats into small drops, and **pancreatic juice** and **intestinal juice** complete digestion. **Villi** (finger-like projections) **absorb** the digested food into the blood. |
| **Large intestine** | Absorbs **water** and salts. |
| **Rectum and anus** | Undigested waste is stored and then removed (**egestion**). |

### Digestive glands

- **Salivary glands** (saliva), **liver** (bile, the **largest gland** in the body), **pancreas** (pancreatic juice)

### Teeth

| Type | Number (adult) | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Incisors | 8 | Cutting and biting |
| Canines | 4 | Tearing |
| Premolars | 8 | Chewing and grinding |
| Molars | 12 | Chewing and grinding |
| **Total** | **32** | Milk teeth: **20** |

:::trick
**The food's journey: "My Old Sister Sells Lovely Rasgullas Always"**: Mouth, Oesophagus, Stomach, Small intestine, Large intestine, Rectum, Anus.
**Teeth: "I Can Play Music"**: Incisors (cut), Canines (tear), Premolars and Molars (grind).
:::

:::mumbai
When you eat a **vada pav**: the pav (starch) starts digesting in the **mouth** with saliva. The besan coating (protein) is digested in the **stomach** and small intestine. The oil (fat) is broken down by **bile** in the **small intestine**!
:::

:::play Digestion Relay
Lay out stations around the classroom labelled with the organs. A student is the "food" and must visit them in order, saying what happens at each one. The team that finishes the journey fastest and correctly wins.
:::

:::check
1. Define digestion.
2. Write the parts of the alimentary canal in order.
3. Name the largest gland of the body and its juice.
4. What are villi? What is their function?
:::

## 5. The Respiratory System

- **Breathing:** taking in air (**inhalation**) and giving out air (**exhalation**).
- **Respiration:** the breakdown of food (glucose) **inside cells** to release **energy**.
  **Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy**

### The path of air

**Nostrils → nasal cavity → pharynx → larynx (voice box) → trachea (windpipe) → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli (air sacs) in the lungs**

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Nose** | Hairs and mucus **filter** the dust. The air is warmed and moistened. |
| **Trachea** | **Rings of cartilage** keep it open. |
| **Lungs** | Two spongy organs in the chest, protected by the **rib cage** |
| **Alveoli** | Tiny air sacs where **oxygen enters the blood** and **CO₂ leaves the blood** |
| **Diaphragm** | A dome-shaped muscle below the lungs that helps breathing |

### The breathing mechanism

| Inhalation | Exhalation |
|---|---|
| The diaphragm moves **down** (flattens) | The diaphragm moves **up** (dome shape) |
| The ribs move **up and out** | The ribs move **down and in** |
| The chest gets **bigger**, so air rushes **in** | The chest gets **smaller**, so air is pushed **out** |

- **Breathing in through the nose is better than through the mouth**, because the nose filters, warms and moistens the air.
- An adult breathes about **15–18 times a minute** at rest.

:::trick
**Air's path: "No Nonsense, People Like To Breathe Better Always"**: Nostrils, Nasal cavity, Pharynx, Larynx, Trachea, Bronchi, Bronchioles, Alveoli.
**Breathing = mechanical (lungs). Respiration = chemical (cells).**
:::

:::mumbai
On high-AQI days in Mumbai, doctors advise wearing a **mask** outdoors. Dust and smoke harm the **alveoli** and can cause asthma. Trees on the roadside help to trap the dust.
:::

:::play Balloon Lungs Model
Make a model with a plastic bottle (the chest), a balloon inside (the lung) and a rubber sheet at the bottom (the diaphragm). Pull the sheet down and the balloon fills. Push it up and the balloon shrinks. Students explain what they see.
:::

:::check
1. Differentiate between breathing and respiration.
2. Write the path of air from the nostrils to the alveoli.
3. What is the role of the diaphragm?
4. Why should we breathe through the nose?
:::

## 6. The Circulatory System

- The **circulatory system** carries **oxygen, food, water and hormones** to all the cells, and carries **wastes and CO₂** away.
- Its parts are the **heart**, the **blood vessels** and the **blood**.

### The heart

- A muscular organ about the size of your **fist**, slightly to the **left** of the chest.
- It has **four chambers**: 2 upper **atria** (they receive blood) and 2 lower **ventricles** (they pump blood out).
- **Valves** stop blood from flowing backwards.
- The heart beats about **72 times per minute**. A **stethoscope** is used to hear the heartbeat.

### Blood vessels

| Arteries | Veins | Capillaries |
|---|---|---|
| Carry blood **away** from the heart | Carry blood **to** the heart | Very thin vessels that connect arteries to veins |
| Usually **oxygen-rich** blood | Usually **oxygen-poor** blood | Where the exchange with cells happens |
| Thick, elastic walls | Thin walls, with valves | One cell thick |

- **Exception:** the pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood to the lungs, and the pulmonary vein carries oxygen-rich blood back.

### Blood

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| **Plasma** (a yellowish liquid, about 55%) | Carries food, wastes and hormones |
| **Red blood cells (RBCs)** | Contain **haemoglobin** and carry **oxygen**. They make blood red. |
| **White blood cells (WBCs)** | **Fight germs** (the body's soldiers) |
| **Platelets** | Help blood **clot** at a cut |

- The **pulse** is felt at the wrist. It is the same as the heart rate.

:::trick
**A for Arteries, A for Away from the heart.**
**RBC = Red Bus Carrying oxygen. WBC = White Brave Cops fighting germs. Platelets = Plumbers fixing leaks (cuts)!**
:::

:::play Pulse Party
Students find their pulse at the wrist and count it for 15 seconds × 4. Then they do 20 jumping jacks and count again. Compare: why does it increase? Make a class bar graph of resting and active pulse rates.
:::

:::check
1. How many chambers does the human heart have? Name them.
2. Give two differences between arteries and veins.
3. What is the function of (a) RBCs (b) platelets?
4. What is a stethoscope used for?
:::

## 7. Health and Hygiene

- **Health** is a state of complete **physical, mental and social well-being**, not just the absence of disease (World Health Organization).
- **Hygiene** means the practices that keep us healthy and clean.

### Personal hygiene

Bathe daily, brush twice a day, cut your nails, wash your hands before eating and after using the toilet, wear clean clothes, sleep 8–10 hours, exercise.

### Community hygiene

Proper garbage disposal (wet and dry dustbins), clean drains, no open defecation, no spitting, clean water supply, **don't let water collect** (it breeds mosquitoes).

### Diseases

| Type | How they spread | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Communicable (infectious)** | Germs pass from person to person through air, water, food, insects or contact | Cold, flu, TB, cholera, typhoid, malaria, dengue, COVID-19 |
| **Non-communicable** | Don't spread from person to person | Diabetes, heart disease, cancer, deficiency diseases |

| Disease | Germ or cause | Spread by |
|---|---|---|
| Malaria | Protozoan (Plasmodium) | Female **Anopheles** mosquito |
| Dengue | Virus | **Aedes** mosquito (bites in the daytime, breeds in clean stagnant water) |
| Cholera, typhoid | Bacteria | Contaminated food and water |
| Tuberculosis (TB) | Bacteria | Air (coughing, sneezing) |
| Common cold | Virus | Air and contact |

### Balanced diet

- A **balanced diet** contains carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, fibre (roughage) and water **in the right amounts**.
- **Deficiency diseases:** scurvy (lack of vitamin C), night blindness (vitamin A), rickets (vitamin D), anaemia (iron), goitre (iodine).

### Vaccination

- **Vaccines** protect us from diseases such as polio, measles, tetanus, hepatitis B and BCG (for TB).

:::trick
**"Aedes in the Afternoon (daytime), Anopheles At night."** Dengue mosquitoes bite in the daytime, malaria mosquitoes at night.
**Vitamins and their diseases: "A–Night, C–Scurvy, D–Rickets"** (A night sky, C sea-scurvy pirates, D for "damaged bones").
:::

:::mumbai
During the **monsoon**, cases of **dengue, malaria and leptospirosis** rise in Mumbai because water collects in tyres, flower pots, construction sites and on terraces. The BMC runs "dry day" drives. Students can check their homes every Sunday for stagnant water!
:::

:::play Germ Glitter Game
Put a little glitter (the "germs") on one student's hands. They shake hands with 3 friends. Watch the glitter spread! Then wash with water only, and then with soap. Which removes more? That's why we wash our hands with soap for 20 seconds.
:::

2. Differentiate between communicable and non-communicable diseases, with examples.
3. Name the mosquito that spreads (a) malaria (b) dengue.
4. Name the deficiency disease caused by a lack of vitamin C and of iodine.

## 8. Habitat and Adaptation

- **Habitat:** the natural home of a living thing, where it gets food, water, shelter and suitable conditions.
- **Adaptation:** special features that help an organism **survive** in its habitat.
- **Biotic components:** living things (plants, animals, microbes). **Abiotic components:** non-living things (air, water, soil, sunlight, temperature).

### Types of habitats and adaptations

| Habitat | Example | Adaptations |
|---|---|---|
| **Desert** (hot and dry) | **Camel** | Long legs (stay away from the hot sand), wide padded feet (don't sink), stores fat in its **hump**, rarely sweats, can go without water for days |
| | **Cactus** | Leaves become **spines** (less water loss), a thick green stem does photosynthesis and stores water, a thick waxy coating, deep roots |
| **Polar** (very cold) | **Polar bear** | Thick white fur (warmth and camouflage), a thick fat layer, a strong sense of smell |
| | **Penguin** | Thick skin and fat, huddles in groups, webbed feet for swimming |
| **Aquatic (water)** | **Fish** | A streamlined body, **gills** for breathing, fins and a tail, slippery scales |
| | **Lotus, water lily** | Floating broad leaves with **stomata on the upper surface**, hollow stems, a waxy coating |
| **Mountain** (cold) | **Yak, mountain goat** | Thick fur, strong hooves for climbing |
| | **Pine, deodar trees** | **Cone-shaped**, so snow slides off, with needle-like leaves |
| **Grassland** | **Lion, deer** | A lion's colour helps it hide in dry grass. A deer has long ears and eyes on the sides to watch for danger, and strong legs to run fast. |
| **Forest (tree-dwelling)** | **Monkey** | A long tail and gripping hands for climbing |
| **Air (flying)** | **Birds** | **Hollow light bones**, feathers, wings, a streamlined body |

### Plant adaptations

- **Climbers:** use tendrils (pea, grapes)
- **Insectivorous plants** (pitcher plant, Venus flytrap): catch insects to get the **nitrogen** missing from their soil
- **Mangroves:** breathing roots (**pneumatophores**) that grow **above** the salty, muddy ground

:::trick
**"Every animal has a superpower for its home!"** Camel = water tank (fat in the hump), fish = built-in snorkel (gills), polar bear = winter jacket (fur + fat), birds = lightweight backpack (hollow bones).
:::

:::mumbai
Mumbai's **mangroves** (at Mahim, Vikhroli and Airoli) have **breathing roots** that poke up from the salty mud, and they protect the city from floods and storms. Mumbai also has **flamingos** at Sewri and Thane Creek every winter. Their long legs help them wade, and their bent beaks filter food from the mud!
:::

:::
