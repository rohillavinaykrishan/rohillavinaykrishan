## 1. Introduction to Chemistry

- **Chemistry** is the branch of science that studies **matter**: what it is made of (**composition**), its **properties** and the **changes** it goes through.
- **Antoine Lavoisier** is called the **Father of Modern Chemistry**.
- **Ancient India:** Acharya **Nagarjuna** worked with metals and medicines. **Kanad** proposed the idea of tiny particles called "**parmanu**" (atoms).

### Importance of chemistry

| Field | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Food | Fertilisers, pesticides, preservatives |
| Health | Medicines, antiseptics, vaccines, soaps |
| Clothes | Synthetic fibres (nylon, polyester), dyes |
| Home | Detergents, cooking gas, plastics, cement |
| Industry | Fuels, metals, glass, paper |

### The dark side of chemistry

- Pollution of air, water and soil. Plastic waste. Harmful pesticides. Explosives and chemical weapons.
- We must use chemicals **wisely and safely**.

### Laboratory apparatus

| Apparatus | Use |
|---|---|
| Test tube | Heating or mixing small amounts of substances |
| Beaker | Holding and mixing liquids |
| Conical (Erlenmeyer) flask | Mixing liquids without spilling |
| Round-bottom flask | Heating liquids evenly |
| Funnel | Pouring liquids into narrow openings and filtering |
| Measuring cylinder | Measuring the volume of liquids |
| Burner (Bunsen burner or spirit lamp) | Heating |
| Tripod stand and wire gauze | Supporting apparatus while heating |
| Test tube holder | Holding a hot test tube |
| China dish | Evaporating liquids |
| Watch glass | Covering a beaker, or evaporating small amounts |
| Glass rod | Stirring |
| Tongs | Holding hot objects |

### Lab safety rules

1. Never taste or directly smell chemicals.
2. Point the mouth of a test tube **away** from yourself and others while heating.
3. Wear an apron and goggles, and tie long hair back.
4. Never add water to acid. **Add acid to water** slowly.
5. Wash your hands after the experiment.

:::trick
**"Do what you oughta, add acid to water!"**
**Kanad = "Kan" (particle)**: the Indian sage who thought about atoms.
:::

## 2. Elements, Compounds, Symbols and Formulae

- An **element** is a pure substance made of **only one kind of atom**. It cannot be broken into simpler substances. For example: iron, oxygen, gold. There are **118** known elements.
- A **compound** forms when **two or more elements combine chemically in a fixed ratio**. For example: water (H₂O), common salt (NaCl), carbon dioxide (CO₂).
- **Atom:** the smallest particle of an element. **Molecule:** the smallest particle of a substance that can exist on its own (O₂, H₂O).

### Kinds of elements

| Metals | Non-metals | Metalloids |
|---|---|---|
| Shiny (lustrous) | Dull (except iodine) | Some properties of both |
| Good conductors of heat and electricity | Poor conductors (except graphite) | |
| Malleable (beaten into sheets) and ductile (drawn into wires) | Brittle | |
| Solid (except **mercury**, a liquid) | Solid, liquid (**bromine**) or gas | |
| Iron, copper, gold, aluminium | Oxygen, carbon, sulphur, hydrogen | Silicon, boron, arsenic |

### Symbols of elements

| Element | Symbol | Element | Symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen | H | Sodium (Natrium) | **Na** |
| Helium | He | Potassium (Kalium) | **K** |
| Carbon | C | Iron (Ferrum) | **Fe** |
| Nitrogen | N | Copper (Cuprum) | **Cu** |
| Oxygen | O | Silver (Argentum) | **Ag** |
| Sulphur | S | Gold (Aurum) | **Au** |
| Chlorine | Cl | Lead (Plumbum) | **Pb** |
| Calcium | Ca | Mercury (Hydrargyrum) | **Hg** |
| Magnesium | Mg | Tin (Stannum) | **Sn** |
| Aluminium | Al | Zinc | Zn |
| Phosphorus | P | Iodine | I |

- A symbol is **one capital letter**, or **one capital + one small letter**.
- Some symbols come from **Latin names** (shown in brackets above).

### Valency

- **Valency** is the combining capacity of an element (how many "hands" it has to join with others).

| Valency 1 | Valency 2 | Valency 3 |
|---|---|---|
| H, Na, K, Ag, Cl | O, Mg, Ca, Zn, Cu (also 1), Fe (also 3) | Al, N, Fe (also 2) |

**Radicals** (groups of atoms that act as one unit):

| Radical | Formula | Valency |
|---|---|---|
| Hydroxide | OH | 1 |
| Nitrate | NO₃ | 1 |
| Ammonium | NH₄ | 1 |
| Carbonate | CO₃ | 2 |
| Sulphate | SO₄ | 2 |

### Writing formulae: the criss-cross method

1. Write the symbols side by side (the metal first).
2. Write each valency above its symbol.
3. **Criss-cross** the valencies and write them as subscripts.
4. Simplify if possible, and don't write the subscript 1.

**Example:** Aluminium oxide: Al has valency 3 and O has valency 2 → **Al₂O₃**
**Example:** Calcium chloride: Ca has valency 2 and Cl has valency 1 → **CaCl₂**
**Example:** Magnesium oxide: Mg has valency 2 and O has valency 2 → Mg₂O₂ → simplify → **MgO**

### Common formulae

| Compound | Formula |
|---|---|
| Water | H₂O |
| Common salt (sodium chloride) | NaCl |
| Carbon dioxide | CO₂ |
| Ammonia | NH₃ |
| Methane | CH₄ |
| Calcium carbonate (chalk, limestone) | CaCO₃ |
| Sodium hydroxide (caustic soda) | NaOH |
| Sulphuric acid | H₂SO₄ |
| Hydrochloric acid | HCl |

:::trick
**Latin symbols, a story to remember:** "**Na**ughty **K**id **Fe**ll in **Cu**p of **Ag**ra **Au**nty, **Pb** (plumber) **Hg** (hugged) **Sn** (snake)."
Na = sodium, K = potassium, Fe = iron, Cu = copper, Ag = silver, Au = gold, Pb = lead, Hg = mercury, Sn = tin.
**Criss-cross = "swap your hands"**: the valency of one becomes the subscript of the other.
:::

:::play Valency Handshake

:::

4. Which metal is a liquid at room temperature?
:::

## 3. Matter: Physical and Chemical Changes

- **Matter** is anything that has mass and occupies space. Its three states are **solid, liquid and gas**.
- **Interconversion:** heating changes solid → liquid → gas, and cooling reverses it.

### Physical change vs chemical change

| Physical change | Chemical change |
|---|---|
| **No new substance** forms | A **new substance** forms |
| Usually **reversible** | Usually **irreversible** |
| Only the shape, size or state changes | The composition changes |
| Little or no energy change | Heat, light or sound is often given out or taken in |
| Melting ice, dissolving sugar, tearing paper, magnetising iron | Burning paper, rusting iron, cooking food, curdling milk, ripening fruit |

### Signs of a chemical change

A change of colour, gas bubbles, a smell, heat or light, or a solid forming (precipitate).

### Rusting

- Iron + oxygen + moisture → **rust** (iron oxide, reddish-brown)
- **Prevent it by** painting, oiling or greasing, or **galvanising** (coating with zinc).

### Desirable and undesirable changes

- **Desirable:** cooking food, ripening fruit, making curd
- **Undesirable:** rusting, food going bad, milk going sour by accident

:::trick
**Physical = "Fake makeover"**: it looks different, but it's still the same thing inside.
**Chemical = "Real transformation"**: a brand-new substance, and you usually can't go back. (You can't un-cook a vada pav!)
:::

:::mumbai
Iron railings and the gates of buildings near the sea in Mumbai **rust faster**. Salty, moist sea air speeds up rusting. That's why they are painted again every year before the monsoon!
:::

:::play Physical or Chemical? Stand-Up Game
The teacher calls out a change ("Lighting a diya!", "Melting butter!", "Making curd!"). Students stand up for chemical and stay seated for physical. Anyone who's wrong explains the correct answer.
:::

:::check
1. Give two differences between physical and chemical changes.
2. Classify: (a) dissolving salt in water (b) burning a candle's wick (c) rusting.
3. What three things are needed for rusting?
4. Give two ways to prevent rusting.
:::

## 4. Pure Substances, Mixtures and Separation

- A **pure substance** has a fixed composition and fixed properties: elements and compounds.
- A **mixture** has two or more substances mixed in **any ratio**, with **no chemical combination**. Each part keeps its own properties.

| Mixture | Compound |
|---|---|
| Parts mixed in **any proportion** | Elements combined in a **fixed proportion** |
| Parts keep their own properties | The properties are new and different |
| Can be separated by **physical** methods | Can be separated only by **chemical** methods |
| Example: air, salt water, soil | Example: water, salt, CO₂ |

- **Homogeneous mixture:** the same throughout (salt in water, air)
- **Heterogeneous mixture:** not uniform, so you can see the parts (sand in water, oil in water)

### Methods of separation

| Method | Principle (why it works) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hand picking | Different size and colour | Stones from rice and dal |
| Winnowing | Light husk is blown away by wind, while heavy grain falls | Husk from grain |
| Threshing | Beating to separate grain from the stalks | Wheat |
| Sieving | Different particle sizes | Flour from bran, sand at construction sites |
| Magnetic separation | One part is magnetic | Iron filings from sand |
| Sedimentation and decantation | The heavy solid settles, then the liquid is poured off | Muddy water |
| Filtration | Insoluble solid particles can't pass through filter paper | Sand from water, tea leaves |
| Evaporation | The liquid turns into vapour, leaving the dissolved solid | Salt from seawater |
| Sublimation | One part changes directly from solid to gas | Camphor or ammonium chloride from salt |
| Distillation | Liquids with different boiling points (or a solid from a liquid, keeping the liquid) | Pure water from salt water |
| Separating funnel | Liquids that don't mix (immiscible), with different densities | Oil and water |
| Churning | Lighter fat separates when spun | Butter from curd or milk |

- **Filtrate:** the clear liquid that passes through the filter. **Residue:** the solid left on the filter paper.

:::trick
**Your kitchen is a chemistry lab!**
Tea strainer = filtration. Chhalni (sieve) = sieving. Making butter (makhan) = churning. Picking stones from dal = hand picking.
:::

:::mumbai
Salt pans near **Bhandup, Wadala and Uran** make salt by **evaporating seawater** in the sun. The water evaporates and salt crystals are left behind!
:::

:::play Separation Challenge
Give each team a mixture in a cup: sand + iron filings + salt + water. Teams plan the order of separation (magnet → filter → evaporate) and present it. The best plan with the correct principles wins.
:::

:::check
1. Give two differences between a mixture and a compound.
2. Name the method of separation: (a) husk from grain (b) iron pins from sand (c) camphor from salt.
3. What are the filtrate and the residue?
4. How is salt obtained from seawater?
:::

## 5. Air and Its Constituents

- **Air** is a **mixture** of gases. It surrounds the Earth as the **atmosphere**.

### Composition of air (by volume)

| Gas | Percentage |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen | about 78% |
| Oxygen | about 21% |
| Argon and other noble gases | about 0.9% |
| Carbon dioxide | about 0.03–0.04% |
| Water vapour, dust, smoke | Varies |

### Why is air a mixture?

1. Its composition varies from place to place.
2. Its gases can be separated by physical methods (such as the fractional distillation of liquid air).
3. Each gas keeps its own properties.
4. No heat or light is given out when the gases are mixed.

### Importance of each gas

| Gas | Use |
|---|---|
| **Oxygen** | Needed for **breathing (respiration)** and **burning (combustion)**, and used in hospitals and by divers |
| **Nitrogen** | Slows down burning, plants need it to make proteins, used to make fertilisers and to keep food fresh in chip packets |
| **Carbon dioxide** | Plants need it for **photosynthesis**, used in fire extinguishers and fizzy drinks |
| **Water vapour** | Forms clouds and rain |
| **Noble gases** | Argon is used in bulbs and neon in advertisement signs |

### Experiments with air

- **Air has mass:** a balloon filled with air weighs more than an empty one.
- **Air occupies space:** push an upside-down glass into water. The water doesn't fill the glass, because air is inside.
- **Oxygen is needed for burning:** cover a burning candle with a glass jar. It goes out once the oxygen is used up.
- **Lime water test:** carbon dioxide turns lime water **milky**.

### Air pollution

- **Causes:** vehicle smoke, factory smoke, burning garbage, firecrackers, construction dust
- **Effects:** breathing problems (asthma), acid rain, global warming
- **Prevention:** public transport, CNG vehicles, planting trees, no burning of garbage

:::trick
**"78 + 21 = 99, Nitrogen and Oxygen are the big two!"**
**Nitrogen is the "calm friend" who slows down fire. Oxygen is the "excited friend" who helps fire burn!**
:::

:::mumbai
Mumbai's **AQI (Air Quality Index)** gets worse in winter, around Diwali and near construction sites. Students can check the AQI on a phone app for one week and make a chart. Which day was worst, and why?
:::

:::play Candle Under the Jar
Light 3 candles of the same size. Cover one with a small glass, one with a big glass, and leave one open. Which goes out first? (The small glass, because it has the least oxygen.) Students write down their observation and conclusion.
:::

:::check
1. Name the two main gases in air and their percentages.
2. Give two reasons why air is a mixture.
3. Which gas turns lime water milky?
4. Write two uses of nitrogen.
:::

## 6. Water

- Water covers about **71%** of the Earth's surface. About **97%** of it is salty seawater. Only about **3%** is fresh water, and most of that is frozen in ice caps and glaciers.
- Water is a **compound** of hydrogen and oxygen (**H₂O**), with hydrogen and oxygen in the ratio **2 : 1 by volume**.

### Sources of water

- **Rain** (the purest natural form), rivers, lakes, ponds, wells, springs, seas and oceans, groundwater

### Water is the "universal solvent"

- Water dissolves more substances than any other liquid.
- **Solute:** the substance that dissolves (sugar). **Solvent:** the liquid it dissolves in (water). **Solution** = solute + solvent.
- **Saturated solution:** no more solute can dissolve at that temperature. Heating usually lets more solute dissolve.

### Water cycle

Evaporation (seas, rivers) → condensation (forming clouds) → precipitation (rain) → collection (rivers, groundwater) → evaporation again

### Purifying water (from a city supply)

**Screening/sedimentation → loading (adding alum) → filtration (sand beds) → chlorination (kills germs) → storage and supply**

### Purifying water at home

Boiling, filtering, adding chlorine tablets, alum, RO/UV water purifiers

### Hard and soft water

- **Soft water** lathers easily with soap (rainwater).
- **Hard water** doesn't lather easily, because it contains dissolved calcium and magnesium salts.

### Saving water

Close taps, fix leaks, use a bucket instead of a shower, **harvest rainwater**, reuse RO waste water for mopping.

:::trick
**Water cycle = "E-C-P-C": "Every Cloud Pours Carefully"**: Evaporation, Condensation, Precipitation, Collection.
:::

:::mumbai
Mumbai's drinking water comes from lakes outside the city, such as **Vaitarna, Tansa, Bhatsa and Vihar**. The BMC treats it at plants such as **Bhandup**, one of Asia's biggest water treatment plants. In years with poor rain, the BMC announces **water cuts**. That's why saving water matters!
:::

:::play Water Detective
Each student counts how many litres of water their family uses in one day (one bucket ≈ 15 L). The class adds up all the totals, then plans 5 ways to save 20%.
:::

:::check
1. What percentage of the Earth's water is fresh water?
2. Why is water called a universal solvent?
3. Write the steps of the water cycle in order.
4. What is hard water?
:::
